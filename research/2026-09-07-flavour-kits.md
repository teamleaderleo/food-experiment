# Flavour Kit Rotation — 2026-09-07

## Goal

Build a small sauce/condiment library that can make the same efficient grocery backbone taste like genuinely different meals.

The backbone stays boring and reusable:

- rice / pasta / tortillas / naan
- chicken / pork / beef / tofu / eggs
- frozen spinach / peas / pepper-onion mixes
- milk / yogurt / skyr where useful
- tomato concentrate
- iodized salt

The flavour kit should do the identity work for roughly **$0.20–$1.50 per meal**.

The best kit is one that:

1. tastes strong enough to define the meal,
2. keeps well enough to avoid waste,
3. works with several proteins/starches,
4. takes almost no extra prep,
5. gives a clear reason to want this meal again.

## Storage tiers

### Tier A — very low-friction fridge/pantry anchors

These are ideal permanent members of the flavour library:

- soy sauce
- rice vinegar
- gochujang
- doubanjiang / chili-bean paste
- tahini
- sriracha / chili sauce
- Japanese curry roux

Manufacturer guidance is especially friendly for several of these:

- Kikkoman recommends refrigerating opened sauces for best quality and generally using them within about **1 month**; regular soy sauce is particularly stable, though refrigeration keeps quality higher.
- Kikkoman rice vinegar can also be refrigerated and used within about **1 month** for best quality.
- S&B says opened curry roux can be kept **airtight in the refrigerator for up to 3 months**.
- Lee Kum Kee advises refrigeration after opening for product quality.

Fermented pastes like gochujang and chili-bean paste are naturally suited to a long-lived condiment library; follow the purchased tub/jar label for exact storage.

### Tier B — planned-rotation jars

These are excellent, but buy them when several meals in the coming week can use them:

- salsa
- garlic sauce / toum
- Indian curry paste
- Thai curry paste

### Tier C — short-window flavour splurges

- pesto
- fresh cilantro / basil / parsley
- fresh pico de gallo

Classico's manufacturer guidance is unusually short: opened pesto should be refrigerated and used within **5 days**. That makes pesto a planned-week ingredient rather than a permanent lonely jar. Freeze small portions if the purchased product/recipe handles freezing acceptably.

## Ten flavour kits

Prices are dated Toronto/Mississauga-area online anchors in CAD. The "identity cost" is the sauce/paste/condiment cost only, before the shared starch/protein/vegetable backbone.

### 1. Japanese curry comfort kit

**Identity ingredients**

- S&B Golden Curry roux
- optional soy sauce
- optional grated apple / honey if already around

**Current anchor**

- S&B Golden Curry, 92 g — **$3.97**
- ~18–23 g roux per large meal = **~$0.78–$0.99**

**Best backbone**

- rice
- chicken thigh, pork, beef, tofu, or rotisserie chicken
- peas / onion / carrot
- egg optional

**Why it wins**

One block turns cheap rice and freezer ingredients into a coherent comfort-food dinner. S&B's own formulation already contains curry spices, MSG, guanylate and inosinate, so it does an enormous amount of flavour work by itself.

**Storage**

Opened block: airtight container, refrigerated, **use within 3 months** per S&B.

**Palatability score:** very high
**Convenience score:** exceptional
**Repeat risk:** low; easy to change protein/toppings

### 2. Indian curry-paste kit

**Identity ingredients**

- Patak's Madras / mild curry / korma paste
- yogurt or milk as optional creamy finish
- cilantro only when desired

**Current anchor**

- Patak's 284 mL spice pastes: current sale examples **$2.98**, regular examples around **$5.27–$5.97**
- 30 mL in a meal = roughly **$0.31 sale / $0.56–$0.63 regular**

**Best backbone**

- chicken thigh/breast
- ground meat
- lentils
- rice or naan
- spinach / peas

**Why it wins**

A jar supplies a complex spice blend without measuring ten spices. Particularly strong with chicken + rice/naan, or meat + a small lentil amount.

**Storage note**

Follow the jar label after opening. Patak's specifically says its spice pastes must be cooked; finished cooked recipes can be frozen, so batching curry is a useful waste-control strategy.

**Palatability score:** very high
**Convenience score:** high

**Palatability score:** extremely high if you like Korean flavours

### 4. Mapo / Sichuan chili-bean kit

**Identity ingredients**

- doubanjiang / chili-bean sauce
- soy sauce
- optional Sichuan pepper
- optional chili oil

**Current anchor**

- Lee Kum Kee Chili Bean Sauce (Toban Djan), ~227 g — **$4.47**
- 20 g = **~$0.39**

**Best backbone**

- soft tofu + ground pork/beef

**Why it wins**

This may be the best low-chew meal family in the whole project: silky tofu, minced meat, sauce and rice. Doubanjiang supplies fermented savouriness, salt and heat, so the ingredient list can stay short.

**Storage**

Lee Kum Kee advises refrigeration after opening for quality.

**Palatability score:** elite
**Eating-throughput score:** elite

### 5. Soy-vinegar-sriracha universal kit

**Identity ingredients**

- reduced-sodium soy sauce
- rice vinegar
- sriracha
- optional sesame oil / sugar / garlic

**Current anchors**

- Great Value reduced-sodium soy sauce: about **$0.50/100 mL**
- Pearl River Bridge rice vinegar: **$3.27/500 mL (~$0.65/100 mL)**
- Lee Kum Kee sriracha: **$5.97/445 mL (~$1.37/100 mL)**

Example 15 mL soy + 15 mL vinegar + 10 mL sriracha = roughly **$0.31**.

**Best backbone**

Everything:

- chicken rice bowl
- pork rice
- tofu
- noodles
- fried eggs
- frozen vegetables

**Why it wins**

This is the neutral "I have food but no plan" sauce. Acid + salt/umami + heat can rescue almost any starch/protein combination.

**Storage**

Kikkoman recommends opened sauces refrigerated for best quality and generally used within about a month; regular soy sauce itself is particularly shelf-stable. Rice vinegar likewise keeps quality well refrigerated.

**Palatability score:** high
**Flexibility score:** maximal

## Starter flavour-library cart

A very strong first condiment purchase would be:

- reduced-sodium soy sauce
- rice vinegar
- gochujang
- S&B Golden Curry
- chili-bean sauce / doubanjiang
- tahini
- sriracha

At current mainstream-store anchors this is roughly a **$30–35 initial condiment buy-in**, but the jars/tubs collectively cover dozens of meals.

Then buy **one short-window identity kit at a time**:

- pesto week
- salsa/chipotle week
- Indian curry week
- Thai curry week

This keeps the fridge from becoming a cemetery of half-used sauces.

## Suggested 7-dinner rotation from one grocery backbone

The same chicken/pork/tofu/eggs/rice/pasta/freezer-veg inventory could become:

1. Japanese curry chicken rice
2. Mapo tofu + pork rice
3. Gochujang beef/pork rice bowl + egg
4. Spicy rosé/pesto chicken pasta
5. Shawarma chicken rice + tahini/toum
6. Mexican chicken/bean tortillas + salsa/chipotle crema
7. Peanut-chili noodles with chicken/tofu

That is enough sensory separation that "same ingredients every day" should feel much less repetitive.

## Key storage finding

There are really two types of flavour ingredients:

### Permanent library

Fermented/salty/acidic/powdered products with low waste risk:

- soy sauce
- vinegar
- gochujang
- doubanjiang
- curry roux
- tahini
- sriracha

### Scheduled guests

Fresh or short-open-life products:

- pesto
- fresh herbs
- fresh salsa/pico
- some creamy refrigerated sauces

The meal plan should be designed around this distinction rather than buying everything simultaneously.

## Current recommendation

Start the library with:

> **soy sauce + rice vinegar + gochujang + Golden Curry + doubanjiang + tahini + sriracha**

Those seven items create an absurd amount of variation for a modest up-front spend and minimal spoilage risk.

Then rotate one temporary flavour family each week.
