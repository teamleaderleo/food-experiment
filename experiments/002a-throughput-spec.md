# Experiment 002A: Palatability + Throughput Spec

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D constraint for Ultimate Meal

## Core idea

Price-per-calorie and price-per-protein are only half the problem.

The Ultimate Meal also has to deliver a large bulking day with **low eating friction**. A mathematically perfect meal that requires an hour of chewing, creates extreme fullness, or becomes gross halfway through loses.

Track two different kinds of throughput:

1. **Eating throughput** — calories and protein delivered per minute of active eating/drinking.
2. **Digestive throughput** — how quickly comfortable appetite returns after the meal or meal window.

These are practical R&D metrics, not claims that digestion can be reduced to one exact clock time.

## New optimization hierarchy

Optimize roughly in this order:

1. palatability
2. comfortable calorie delivery
3. protein delivery
4. eating throughput
5. digestive comfort / appetite recovery
6. fibre + micronutrient coverage
7. active prep time
8. cost

Saving another dollar is a bad trade if it turns dinner into a chewing project.

## Eating-throughput metrics

Record for each prototype:

- total calories
- total protein
- total fibre
- cooked / ready-to-eat mass in grams
- active eating time in minutes
- active drinking time in minutes
- **kcal per minute consumed**
- **protein grams per minute consumed**
- **kcal per 100 g ready-to-eat food**
- number of separate components
- number of times eating had to be paused because of fullness or palate fatigue

### Working targets

These are design targets to test, not personal medical targets.

- savoury solid component should ideally be finishable in roughly **15–25 minutes** of active eating
- sweet/liquid component should ideally take roughly **5–10 minutes**
- an entire engineered day should aim for **well under an hour of active consumption**
- aim for enough moisture/sauce that chewing never becomes work
- calorie density should remain high enough that the meal does not become a multi-kilogram bowl

## Digestive-throughput measurements

After the main meal or meal window, record subjective fullness / desire to eat again at:

- immediately after
- 60 minutes
- 120 minutes
- 180 minutes

Simple scale:

- 1 = hungry / could eat easily
- 2 = comfortable, appetite possible
- 3 = neutral
- 4 = quite full
- 5 = stuffed / food sounds bad

Also record:

- bloating
- reflux / heaviness
- gas
- sleepiness
- thirst
- whether training or normal activity feels comfortable

The goal is **high calories without wrecking the next eating opportunity**.

## Important design implications

### Fibre has an optimum, not a maximum

The earlier 55–65 g fibre drafts are useful as a nutritional upper-bound experiment. They may be terrible bulking meals.

For the first throughput prototypes, aim closer to a conventional high-fibre day — roughly **30–45 g** — and only increase if eating volume and digestion stay easy.

Cheap pulses remain valuable, but they should not automatically dominate the entire protein budget just because they win dollars-per-gram.

### Use low-chew calories deliberately

High-value tools:

- milk
- yogurt / skyr
- smooth sauces
- olive oil mixed into food
- peanut butter
- cooked-down lentils
- soy granules rather than large chewy chunks
- well-cooked pasta or rice
- blended fruit / berries

### Keep fat strategically distributed

Fat is excellent for raising calorie density with very little chewing or food volume. Very fatty meals can also sit heavily and delay stomach emptying.

R&D rule: use enough fat to make calories easy, then test whether spreading some of it into the liquid/sweet component improves comfort compared with dumping all of it into the savoury bowl.

### Reduce physical bulk before reducing nutrients

If a prototype is too big:

1. replace some whole-legume volume with soy granules, dairy, eggs, chicken or whey
2. replace some watery vegetables with smaller high-value portions
3. keep berries / colourful produce as targeted micronutrient modules
4. use oil / nut butter to restore calories
5. keep a sensible amount of fibre instead of chasing the largest possible number

## Palatability engineering

A prototype should have deliberate variation across the eating window.

### Savoury component

Target:

- high sauce ratio
- soft starch
- finely textured protein
- enough salt / acidity / umami
- one small crisp/fresh element for contrast
- minimal dry chewing

Leading form: **bolognese mutant**

- pasta
- red lentils cooked into sauce
- soy granules
- smaller real-meat component
- tomato
- finely chopped spinach / peas / carrot
- olive oil
- parmesan or another cheese only when price/taste justifies it

This should eat dramatically quicker than a giant bowl of separate beans, rice, chicken pieces, seeds and raw vegetables.

### Sweet / drinkable component

Leading form: **bulk shake / pudding**

- milk
- oats, blended or very soft
- peanut butter
- berries
- flax
- optional whey / yogurt

This moves calories, calcium, fruit and some fibre into a form with almost zero chewing.

## One-object rule is demoted

The original joke was to compress the whole day into one abomination.

For actual performance, **two coordinated components count as one engineered meal system**:

1. one savoury soft-food main
2. one drinkable or spoonable sweet component

This will probably beat a literal single bowl on palatability, eating speed and digestion while preserving the spirit of the experiment.

## Price-floor warning

The wholesale lentil + soy price-floor model is a useful lower bound. It should not become the default recipe until it proves itself on:

- chewing time
- cooked mass
- flavour fatigue
- digestive comfort
- appetite recovery

The true optimum is likely slightly more expensive and dramatically easier to eat.

## First throughput prototype target

Design a two-component day around:

- **3,000–3,500 kcal**
- **180–220 g protein**
- **30–45 g fibre**
- strong micronutrient breadth
- **<35 minutes total active eating/drinking time** as an initial performance target
- comfortable appetite recovery within a few hours
- price target around **$9–12 CAD/day** before chasing the wholesale mathematical floor

## Verdict criterion

The Ultimate Meal wins when the reaction after finishing is:

> I could actually live like this.

rather than:

> I successfully completed the food challenge.
