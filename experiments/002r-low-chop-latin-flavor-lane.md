# Experiment 002R: Low-Chop Latin Flavor Lane

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** flavor-system R&D

## Goal

Capture the fresh, acidic, chile-lime-cilantro flavor profile the user associates with Mexican / broader Latin-American food **without making hand-chopping vegetables a recurring obligation**.

Pico de gallo itself is Mexican rather than South American, but the underlying craving is broader: bright tomato, onion, chile, lime, herbs, creamy dairy/avocado, beans, tortillas, charred meat, pickles and hot sauce.

The key design rule:

> **Buy or jar the annoying 80%; add one tiny fresh move only when it is actually enjoyable.**

## Freshness hierarchy

### Tier S — buy premade

These are exactly the ingredients where paying a modest premium can be worth it:

- refrigerated pico de gallo
- fresh salsa
- premade guacamole / guacamole cups
- jarred salsa verde
- jarred salsa roja
- chipotle in adobo
- pickled jalapeños
- pickled red onions if available
- prepared chimichurri / herb sauce when a good one is available

Pico is a particularly good candidate for purchased convenience because its entire value comes from fresh chopped texture — which is also the exact labor the user dislikes.

## Tier A — freezer / pantry

Keep these around permanently:

- frozen diced onion
- frozen pepper/onion blend
- frozen corn
- canned black or pinto beans
- canned tomatoes with green chiles
- chipotle in adobo
- salsa verde / salsa roja
- taco seasoning / cumin / Mexican oregano / smoked paprika
- bottled hot sauce
- tortillas
- rice
- shredded cheese
- skyr or yogurt

These ingredients can create a coherent Mexican-ish meal with essentially no cutting board.

## Tier B — one-cut fresh ingredients

A single knife action is acceptable when the payoff is high:

- lime cut in half and squeezed
- avocado cut once and spooned out
- jalapeño only on days when fresh chile feels worth it

The project should distinguish **one-cut freshness** from a full vegetable-prep session.

## Tier C — optional planned chopping day

If once in a while the user actually feels like chopping, make a batch that pays back over several meals:

- pico de gallo
- quick pickled red onion
- cabbage slaw
- cilantro-lime onion mix

Do this only when the mood is there. It is a bonus branch, not a lifestyle requirement.

# Best low-chop meal systems

## 1. Salsa-chicken burrito bowl

### Core

- rice
- chicken thigh or breast cooked with salsa
- black beans, modest amount
- frozen corn
- shredded cheese
- premade pico or salsa
- skyr/yogurt crema
- chipotle/hot sauce

### Why it fits

- almost no knife work
- chicken can be slow-cooker, pressure-cooker, sheet-pan or air-fryer
- beans add fibre without dominating the bowl
- rice remains the easy calorie carrier
- pico supplies the fresh feeling without chopping

### Ultra-lazy crema

Stir lime juice + salt + hot sauce directly into a skyr/yogurt tub and spoon from there.

---

## 2. Chipotle-lime chicken thighs + rice

### Ziploc marinade

- chicken thighs
- chipotle in adobo
- lime juice
- garlic paste/powder
- cumin
- oregano
- oil
- salt

Put everything in the bag, squish from outside, refrigerate/freeze, then air-fry.

### Finish

- rice
- premade pico
- shredded cheese or crema
- pickled jalapeños

This may be the cleanest Mexican-flavor mutation of the current wings/rice workflow.

---

## 3. Giant chicken quesadilla / burrito melt

### Core

- tortillas
- salsa chicken / leftover chicken
- shredded cheese
- modest beans
- salsa or pico
- chipotle

### Why it fits

- very little prep
- pan / air-fryer reheat works well
- cheese + salsa keep the tortilla moist and easy to eat
- leftovers from another meal become the filling

This is a strong **leftover conversion meal**.

---

## 4. Salsa verde chicken rice

### Core

- chicken
- jarred salsa verde
- rice
- frozen corn / peas
- cheese
- yogurt/skyr
- pickled jalapeños

### Why it fits

Salsa verde creates a completely different identity from tomato-chipotle while using almost the same groceries.

A batch of chicken cooked directly in salsa verde can become rice bowls, tortillas and quesadillas for several days.

---

## 5. Black bean + chicken tostada-ish bowl

Skip literal tostadas if crunchy chewing is annoying.

Use:

- rice or crushed tortilla chips
- chicken
- black beans
- premade pico
- cheese
- yogurt/skyr crema
- avocado/guacamole
- hot sauce

This keeps the flavor profile while staying easier to eat than a stack of crisp shells.

# South-American-adjacent flavor branches

If the craving is genuinely broader than Mexican food, several South-American flavor systems are highly compatible with low-chop cooking.

## Peruvian-ish aji amarillo chicken

Use jarred **aji amarillo paste** as the identity ingredient.

- chicken thighs
- aji amarillo paste
- lime
- garlic
- yogurt/mayo/skyr for creaminess
- rice

This gives a bright fruity-chile flavor with almost zero fresh prep.

## Argentinian-ish chimichurri chicken / salmon

Classic chimichurri involves chopped herbs, but the project can simply buy a prepared chimichurri or make a large blender/jar batch rarely.

Use with:

- air-fryer chicken thighs
- salmon
- rice or potatoes

The acid/herb/garlic profile is excellent for large rich meals.

## Colombian-ish sofrito / hogao lane

Traditional hogao involves chopped tomato/onion. For this project, use a prepared sofrito / tomato-onion base or frozen diced onion + canned tomato.

Add to:

- chicken
- rice
- beans
- eggs

The point is flavor identity, not authenticity cosplay.

# Condiment architecture

A strong Latin-flavor shelf/fridge could be only:

- salsa roja
- salsa verde
- chipotle in adobo
- pickled jalapeños
- hot sauce
- taco seasoning / cumin / oregano
- premade pico when wanted
- guacamole cups when wanted
- limes or bottled lime juice as backup

That is enough to make the same chicken/rice/bean/tortilla inventory feel very different across several meals.

## Best convenience spend

The project should happily pay extra for:

1. **premade pico**
2. **premade guacamole**
3. **frozen diced onions / pepper-onion mix**
4. **good jarred salsa**

These purchases directly remove disliked hand work while preserving the flavor identity.

## Avoid pointless purity

Do not insist on homemade pico, homemade salsa, hand-chopped cilantro, hand-diced onion, or handmade tortillas just because they are more traditional.

If the store-bought version tastes good, the project wins.

# Best first tests

1. **Chipotle-lime Ziploc chicken + rice + premade pico + skyr crema**
2. **Salsa verde chicken + rice + cheese + pickled jalapeños**
3. **Leftover chicken giant quesadilla + pico/guac**
4. **Aji amarillo chicken rice** for a genuinely South-American branch

## Current thesis

The Latin lane should feel **fresh without requiring fresh prep**.

The ideal meal is:

> air-fryer / batch chicken + rice/tortilla + one jarred chile sauce + one purchased fresh condiment + dairy/cheese/guac

That gives the brightness, acidity and color associated with pico/salsa-heavy food while keeping the user's actual friction profile intact.
