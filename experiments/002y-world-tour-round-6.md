# Experiment 002Y: World Tour Round 6 — Pelau, Jollof, Rice Identity + Everyday Comfort

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D expansion

## Goal

Keep expanding the food atlas while looking especially for dishes that locals treat as ordinary home food but that feel genuinely new to the project.

This round focused on:

- rice that carries the cuisine identity itself
- one-pot chicken + rice
- creamy chicken dishes that are easier than they look
- soft / low-chew comfort food
- beans and lentils that might actually be worth eating for pleasure
- shortcut bases that turn a labour-heavy dish into a plausible weekday meal

Standing rules still apply:

- chicken / fish / tofu / eggs / dairy first
- pork/beef mostly as flavour-heavy rotation foods
- hand-fuss matters more than elapsed time
- buy prepared aromatics when that is the difference between cooking and ordering
- keep authentic character when possible; simplify workflow rather than flattening every cuisine into generic sauce

# 1. Trinidad & Tobago pelau — one-pot Caribbean chicken + rice

Pelau is a Trinbagonian one-pot meal built around:

- seasoned chicken
- caramelized / browned sugar for colour and flavour
- Caribbean green seasoning
- rice
- pigeon peas
- coconut milk
- Scotch bonnet / chile

CaribbeanPot's wet chicken pelau cooks the final rice/chicken stage in roughly 23–26 minutes once assembled.

## Why it fits

- true one-pot protein + starch
- green seasoning can be bought or batch-made
- coconut milk makes it calorie-friendly
- pigeon peas add fibre without turning dinner into a bean bowl
- leftovers should be excellent
- flavour is very different from arroz con pollo, biryani, kabsa or Hainanese rice

## Project version

Use boneless thighs for eating speed. Keep Caribbean green seasoning in the fridge/freezer. Use canned pigeon peas. A whole Scotch bonnet can perfume the pot without making it brutally hot if left intact.

### Strong hypothesis

Pelau may be one of the best **all-in-one bulk dinners** found so far.

Source: CaribbeanPot — The Ultimate “Wet” Chicken Pelau: https://caribbeanpot.com/the-ultimate-wet-chicken-pelau/

# 2. West African jollof rice — let the rice be the destination

Good Food's baked jollof version is:

- 15 min prep
- 45 min oven time
- freezable
- explicitly compatible with **ready-made sauce**

The red base is peppers + onion + tomato + ginger + garlic + chile, then the rice is seasoned with thyme, curry powder, bay and stock.

## Why it fits

The crucial discovery is that jollof can be treated as a **reusable rice flavour asset** rather than a complicated fresh-vegetable project.

Possible project workflows:

1. buy a good ready-made jollof / red pepper base
2. make a blender batch once and freeze portions
3. use frozen pepper/onion + tomato paste as the lazy fallback

Then pair the rice with:

- air-fryer chicken thighs
- jerk-ish chicken
- fish
- eggs
- tofu experiment

## Phone compatibility

Excellent if baked: once the sauce/rice is assembled, the oven handles most of the cooking.

Sources:
- Good Food — Jollof Rice: https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/jollof-rice
- Good Food — Nigerian-inspired Jollof Rice: https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/jollof-rice-2

# 3. Indonesian opor ayam — mild coconut chicken

Opor ayam is Javanese chicken cooked in a mild coconut-milk sauce with aromatics such as:

- lemongrass
- Indonesian bay leaf
- garlic / shallot
- coriander / cumin and related warm spices
- coconut milk

Daily Cooking Quest lists its version at:

- 15 min prep
- 30 min cook
- 45 min total

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- braise / simmer
- mild enough to be a different lane from spicy curry
- coconut sauce + rice is excellent for calorie delivery
- lemongrass paste can reduce hand-fuss

This feels like a useful bridge between Indonesian food and the broader coconut-curry family.

Source: Daily Cooking Quest — Opor Ayam: https://dailycookingquest.com/opor-ayam.html

# 4. Brazilian chicken stroganoff — everyday 30-minute comfort

Brazilian estrogonofe de frango is a common home-style chicken dish, not special-occasion food.

Brazilian Kitchen Abroad's version is:

- 10 min prep
- 20 min cook
- 30 min total

Core:

- boneless chicken
- onion / Brazilian sofrito or garlic
- tomato sauce
- ketchup
- heavy cream
- rice
- crunchy potato sticks on top

## Why it fits

This is almost perfectly aligned with the project:

- chicken-first
- very soft / saucy
- ordinary supermarket ingredients
- rich enough for the bulk
- rice carries the sauce
- potato sticks give restaurant / childhood-comfort texture with zero prep

## Project tweak

Use thigh meat if breast dries out. Frozen diced onion or prepared sofrito removes most chopping. Cream can be adjusted downward if the rest of the day is already very rich.

Source: Brazilian Kitchen Abroad — Brazilian Chicken Stroganoff: https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-chicken-stroganoff-recipe/

# 5. South Indian lemon rice — 10-minute leftover-rice vacation

Swasthi's lemon rice / chitranna is **under 10 minutes** when rice is already cooked.

The identity comes from a tempering of:

- mustard seed
- small amount of lentil / peanut
- chile
- ginger
- turmeric
- curry leaves
- lemon juice

## Why it is important

This is the opposite of our usual model where chicken gets the flavour treatment.

Here, **plain leftover rice becomes the interesting object**.

Pair with:

- boiled / fried eggs
- air-fryer chicken
- tofu
- yogurt / skyr
- fish

## Project simplification

Keep curry leaves frozen. Use ginger paste. Peanuts are shelf-stable. The only fresh step can be cutting a lemon.

Source: Swasthi's Recipes — Lemon Rice: https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/lemon-rice-recipe/

# 6. South Indian tomato rice — freezer masala cubes are a major workflow idea

Swasthi's tomato rice can be made in:

- pressure cooker
- Instant Pot
- electric rice cooker
- ordinary pot

The especially interesting note is a reader workflow endorsed in the article:

> make a large batch of tomato masala, freeze it in ice trays, then add cubes to the rice cooker later.

## Why it fits

This is exactly the project's emerging **flavour freezer** concept.

A future dinner can become:

- rice
- frozen tomato-masala cubes
- water
- press button

Then add egg / chicken / tofu / yogurt on the side.

## Identity

- tomato
- ginger-garlic
- garam masala / curry powder
- chile
- turmeric
- optional whole spices

Source: Swasthi's Recipes — Tomato Rice: https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/tomato-rice-recipe/

# 7. Curd rice / thayir sadam — savory yogurt-rice comfort

Curd rice is South Indian soft rice mixed with yogurt and finished with a small tempering.

Swasthi's version is:

- 5 min prep
- 30 min rice cook
- 35 min total

The dish itself is mostly:

- very soft cooked rice
- yogurt
- salt
- optional tempering / curry leaves / chile
- optional pickle / podi / pomegranate / cucumber

## Why it is fascinating for this project

This feels like a distant cousin of the skyr-tub habit:

> soft starch + tangy dairy + optional crunchy/spicy finish

It is not a standalone high-protein bulk dinner, but it could be an excellent side / light meal / appetite-friendly recovery meal paired with eggs or chicken.

### Low-fuss branch

Cook rice very soft in the rice cooker, stir in yogurt, use jarred pickle + spice tempering only when desired.

Source: Swasthi's Recipes — Curd Rice: https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/curd-rice-recipe/

# 8. Greek gigantes plaki — canned beans that might actually feel luxurious

Gigantes plaki are giant white beans baked in tomato, vegetables, herbs and a lot of olive oil.

The Mediterranean Dish explicitly says **canned large butter/lima beans are fine** in the quick version; after making the sauce, the beans bake for roughly **20–30 minutes**.

## Why it fits better than many bean ideas

- large creamy beans instead of dry little legumes
- tomato + olive oil + feta makes it feel like dinner
- very soft
- canned beans remove soaking / long simmering
- room-temperature leftovers are plausible
- bread can turn it into a high-calorie meal immediately

## Project simplification

Use frozen mirepoix / onion-carrot-celery mix and garlic paste. Finish with feta and olive oil.

This is a strong test of whether the user dislikes **beans themselves** or merely boring bean preparations.

Source: The Mediterranean Dish — Gigantes Plaki: https://www.themediterraneandish.com/gigantes-plaki-greek-giant-beans/

# 9. Lebanese mujadara — deceptively simple, but the onion labour is real

Mujadara is mostly:

- brown lentils
- rice
- onions
- olive oil
- cumin

Feel Good Foodie's current version is:

- 10 min prep
- 35 min cook
- 45 min total
- 4–5 days refrigerated
- up to 3 months frozen

## Why it is appealing

- extremely cheap pantry food
- rice + lentil combo is substantial
- lots of olive oil makes it bulk-friendly
- yogurt is a natural side
- batch/freezer performance is excellent

## Friction warning

The onions are not decorative. The reference fries them for about **20 minutes with frequent/constant attention** and they carry much of the dish's character.

### Project experiment

Compare:

1. proper fried onions
2. high-quality store-bought crispy onions + extra olive oil

If version 2 is 80–90% as satisfying, mujadara becomes dramatically more compatible with this project.

Source: Feel Good Foodie — Lebanese Mujadara: https://feelgoodfoodie.net/recipe/mujadara/

# 10. Filipino chicken tinola — ginger broth as comfort-food lane

Chicken tinola is a Filipino ginger chicken soup with green papaya or chayote and leafy greens.

Panlasang Pinoy's classic version is:

- 10 min prep
- about 1 hour cook

The cook time is mostly simmering so the chicken becomes tender and flavours the broth.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- ginger-forward rather than soy/coconut/tomato
- extremely soft
- broth makes a large portion easy to eat
- rice can be served alongside
- long elapsed time, low attention density

## Project simplification

Use boneless thighs if bones become annoying, chayote if green papaya is inconvenient, garlic/ginger paste, and a convenient leafy green.

This may be a strong "I want something lighter but still want a proper dinner" meal.

Source: Panlasang Pinoy — Chicken Tinola: https://panlasangpinoy.com/filipino-chicken-tinola-recipe/

# 11. Filipino ginataang manok — spicy coconut chicken

Panlasang Pinoy's 2026 versions frame ginataang manok as a simple chicken dish where chicken simmers in coconut milk / coconut cream and is finished with chile and greens.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- soft braise
- rice-friendly sauce
- rich / calorie-dense
- leafy greens can cook directly in the sauce
- very little need for fresh garnish

This is a useful contrast with opor ayam:

- **opor ayam** = mild Indonesian coconut chicken
- **ginataang manok** = Filipino coconut chicken, often spicier / more direct

# 12. Peruvian ají de gallina — complicated-looking comfort food worth shortcutting

Ají de gallina is cooked shredded chicken in a creamy sauce built around:

- ají amarillo / mirasol
- bread
- chicken stock
- nuts
- evaporated milk / cream
- Parmesan

The traditional-style Peru Delights version is **1 hr 45 min**, so this is not a weekday winner as written.

## Why it still belongs in the atlas

The source describes the dish as three conceptual steps:

1. cook chicken
2. make sauce
3. combine briefly

That means several project shortcuts are obvious:

- rotisserie / leftover chicken
- jarred ají amarillo paste
- pre-grated Parmesan
- blender sauce
- rice cooker rice
- boiled eggs made in advance

## Hypothesis

A thoughtful shortcut version could retain the dish's creamy chile/nut/cheese identity while cutting most of the labour.

Also, the source notes leftovers can be used in sandwiches, empanadas, ravioli etc., so the filling itself behaves like a reusable cooked asset.

Source: Peru Delights — Ají de Gallina: https://perudelights.com/aji-de-gallina-reloaded/

# 13. Peruvian pollo saltado — deliberate active-cooking exception

Pollo saltado is a Peruvian-Chinese stir-fry of chicken, onion, tomato, chile, soy and vinegar, usually eaten with both rice and fries.

Peru Delights lists:

- 20 min prep
- 10 min cook
- 30 min total

## Why it is interesting

This is definitely more hands-on than a braise, but the actual high-attention phase is short.

It also has a hilarious bulking architecture:

> chicken + juicy stir-fry sauce + rice + French fries

## Project simplification

Use pre-cut onion/pepper where possible, frozen or air-fryer fries, ají amarillo paste rather than fresh chile, and chicken thighs/breast strips.

This is the kind of active cooking that can earn its place because the payoff is immediate and the whole meal is done in one short burst.

Source: Peru Delights — Pollo Saltado: https://perudelights.com/pollo-saltado-chicken-stir-fry-sacred-flavors-for-sacred-days/

# 14. Mediterranean fish soup — another boneless frozen-fish application

The Mediterranean Dish updated its fish soup in July 2026. It uses a one-pot broth with cumin, coriander, turmeric, herbs and lemon.

## Why it fits

- frozen boneless white fish works conceptually very well here
- seafood cooks quickly once the broth exists
- soft / low-chew
- warm spice profile is distinct from Korean fish, moqueca and Kerala curry
- bread or rice can raise calories easily

This is another useful reminder that frozen white fish does not have to become a naked fillet.

Source: The Mediterranean Dish — Mediterranean Fish Soup: https://www.themediterraneandish.com/fish-soup-recipe/

# New meta-insight: starch can carry the passport

Until now, many project meals were:

> plain rice + globally flavoured protein

Round 6 reinforces a second powerful model:

> **plain/easy protein + globally flavoured starch**

Examples:

- jollof rice + simple roast chicken
- lemon rice + egg / salmon
- tomato rice + yogurt / chicken
- nasi lemak coconut rice + egg / chicken
- bariis + chicken
- kabsa / mandi
- pelau where the rice and chicken become one system

That means the same air-fryer chicken can feel completely different even when the chicken seasoning barely changes.
