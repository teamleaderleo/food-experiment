# Experiment 002M: Baseline Control + Adjacent Rabbit Holes

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** design-control notes

## The actual thing to beat

The project now has a much stronger control condition:

> **rice cooker + air-fryer chicken wings + one flip + arugula + olive oil + freshly grated garlic**

with optional skyr/seed bowl, pre-strength honey, creatine, and a black-tea/ginger/lemon/hibiscus ritual.

The control is strong because the cooking is parallel and mostly unattended.

A recipe that takes 25 minutes total can still lose badly if those 25 minutes contain 15 minutes of chopping/stirring. Conversely, a 45-minute braise can win if active attention is only 8 minutes and it makes four dinners.

## New benchmark: attention events

Count how many times dinner demands deliberate action.

### Current wing/rice control

Approximate interaction sequence:

1. load/start rice cooker
2. load/start air fryer
3. flip wings
4. grate garlic
5. dump/assemble + oil

That is an absurdly good benchmark: roughly **five meaningful attention events**, with no stovetop babysitting.

New recipes should therefore track:

- active minutes
- number of attention events
- number of cutting-board operations
- cookware/appliances dirtied
- meals produced
- active minutes per meal produced

## Rabbit hole 1: maybe we should mutate the control instead of replacing it

One obvious path is to keep the exact rice-cooker + air-fryer workflow and rotate the flavour identity of the wings / chicken.

### Wing / air-fryer flavour branches

- gochujang-honey-garlic
- soy-five-spice
- Taiwanese three-cup-ish glaze
- shawarma spice + lemon + toum
- adobo-ish soy-vinegar-garlic
- Japanese curry-spiced wings
- lemon-pepper + parmesan
- smoky chipotle-lime
- buffalo + yogurt/skyr ranch-ish dip
- peanut-chili glaze

This is a potentially huge discovery: **variety may be achievable without changing the cooking workflow at all.**

The main downside of wings remains bones / edible yield / slower eating. Therefore every strong wing flavour should eventually be A/B tested against the same flavour on boneless thigh pieces.

## Rabbit hole 2: mutate the rice with almost zero work

The rice cooker can also carry flavour while preserving unattended cooking.

Possible low-friction branches:

- plain white rice
- dashi rice
- ginger-scallion rice
- turmeric/cumin rice
- tomato-bouillon rice
- coconut rice on occasional richer days
- soy-mushroom rice

The rule: if the flavouring adds more than about a minute of active work, it has to earn it.

## Rabbit hole 3: honey before strength training

Honey is a perfectly reasonable convenient carbohydrate source.

A 2025 randomized crossover cycling study found honey supplying carbohydrate during prolonged exercise produced similar carbohydrate oxidation, GI tolerance and exercise-capacity results to a conventional carbohydrate sports product.

That supports **honey as legitimate sports carbohydrate**, but it does **not** establish that raw/unpasteurized honey is superior, nor does it prove that a few-minutes-before dose improves a short strength workout.

Project interpretation:

- keep honey if it tastes good and is an easy calorie/pre-workout ritual
- record the actual grams once
- treat the carbohydrate dose as the important variable
- raw/unpasteurized status is a food preference unless evidence emerges for a relevant performance difference

Reference: PubMed PMID 40675563.

## Rabbit hole 4: creatine timing is probably not worth micromanaging

Long-term evidence has not established a robust pre-vs-post timing advantage.

- a 2021 within-subject resistance-training study found similar hypertrophy/strength gains with creatine immediately before vs immediately after training
- a 2022 8-week athlete trial also directly studied pre- vs post-workout 5 g dosing
- a 2026 pilot crossover trial (n=11) reported better acute lifting performance when a single creatine dose was taken before exercise, but the authors explicitly describe the result as exploratory and in need of larger confirmation

Project interpretation:

> **consistency first; timing is a low-priority optimization.**

Put creatine wherever the daily habit is hardest to forget.

References: PubMed 34610729, 36465581, 42280432.

## Rabbit hole 5: the tea ritual is already useful without turning it into medicine

### Black tea

Health Canada lists brewed black/green tea at roughly **30–50 mg caffeine per 237 mL**.

A randomized hydration trial found black tea did not differ significantly from water on hydration markers under the tested conditions.

So the tea can count as fluid and gives a modest caffeine contribution.

References:

- Health Canada, `Caffeine in Foods`
- PubMed PMID 21450118

### Ginger

The exercise literature is more interesting for repeated gram-level ginger intake than for a couple of flavouring slices in tea.

Systematic-review evidence suggests around ~2 g/day for several days may modestly reduce some exercise-associated soreness, while acute ginger has not shown a clear ergogenic effect.

Therefore frozen ginger slices in tea should primarily be treated as **flavour / ritual** unless the actual dose becomes substantial and deliberate.

References: PubMed 26200194, 19164834, 20418184.

### Hibiscus

Meta-analyses of randomized trials suggest regular hibiscus intake can lower blood pressure, especially in people starting with elevated blood pressure. That is a cardiovascular research observation, not a reason to engineer a performance dose here.

For this project, hibiscus can simply stay because it tastes good and adds a tart/polyphenol-rich plant beverage.

Reference: PubMed 34927694.

## Rabbit hole 6: the 10-minute garlic habit has a real biochemical origin

A classic study found that crushing garlic and allowing it to stand for about 10 minutes before heating protected alliinase-related biological activity that otherwise fell with heating.

This gives the "crush it and wait" idea some biochemical rationale **when garlic will be heated**.

For raw freshly grated garlic eaten with the meal, the project should not treat exactly 10 minutes as a sacred performance rule. Crushing/grating already disrupts the tissue and activates the chemistry.

Reference: PubMed PMID 11238815.

## Rabbit hole 7: kosher salt versus the iodine plan

Canadian salt sold for general household/table use is required to contain 0.01% potassium iodide, and the label must indicate iodide.

However, specialty-use products can differ. The morning kosher salt therefore should **not automatically be counted as the project's iodine plug** until its package is checked.

Practical rule:

- use kosher salt where its texture/taste is useful
- keep a clearly iodized table salt somewhere in the daily cooking system unless the kosher salt label confirms iodine

Reference: CFIA `Labelling requirements for salt`.

## The baseline may be stronger nutritionally than it first looked

The default already has useful roles:

- rice = low-fibre, easy carbohydrate
- wings = protein + energy + extreme palatability
- olive oil = zero-chew unsaturated calories
- arugula = leafy-green / brassica contribution with no cooking
- garlic = flavour + allium plant compound diversity
- skyr = easy second protein pulse + dairy micronutrients
- seeds/flax = fibre, minerals, ALA
- honey = very low-friction carbohydrate
- tea + ginger + lemon + hibiscus = low-calorie plant/beverage diversity

Its weakness is therefore not "this is nutritionally empty." The more accurate weaknesses are:

- exact totals are unknown
- wings are slower to eat than boneless/ground meat
- fibre/micronutrients depend heavily on whether the skyr/seed bowl and enough produce are actually eaten
- iodine remains uncertain if only kosher salt is used
- repeated wing/rice flavour can eventually become boring

## Best near-term experiments

### Experiment A — baseline measurement

Make the current meal exactly as usual and weigh it once.

Do not optimize it during measurement.

### Experiment B — control mutation

Keep rice + air fryer + arugula + oil, but change only the wing seasoning/sauce.

Best first candidates:

1. gochujang-honey-garlic wings
2. shawarma wings + toum
3. soy-five-spice wings

This reveals how much variety can be added for almost zero workflow cost.

### Experiment C — bone penalty

Make the same flavour twice:

- wings
- boneless thigh pieces

Compare:

- eating time
- edible grams
- final-third enjoyment
- active prep
- cleanup
- appetite 2 hours later

### Experiment D — batch challenge

Compare one normal wing/rice day against one 3–4-serving braise such as adobo or rou zao fan using:

> **active minutes per dinner obtained**

This is the fairest contest between the old default and the meal-prep branch.

## Updated project thesis

The goal is no longer to invent a meal that is intellectually superior to wings and rice.

The goal is to find meals that can beat or complement an unusually strong real-world control:

> **five-ish attention events, two unattended appliances, high palatability, easy calories, familiar groceries, almost no cleanup.**

That benchmark should make the project better. Any recipe that survives it has genuinely earned a place.
