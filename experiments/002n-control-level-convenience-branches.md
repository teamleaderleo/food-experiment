# Experiment 002N: Control-Level Convenience Branches

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** convenience-first R&D

## Goal

Find meals that can compete with the current real-world benchmark:

> rice cooker + air fryer + skyr tub

The new rule is deliberately loose. We do not need laboratory precision unless a number would actually change a decision. The meal should win by being delicious, easy, repeatable, and reasonably nutritious.

## Main discovery

There is a whole class of meals that preserve the current workflow instead of replacing it:

1. **one-button rice-cooker meals**
2. **air-fryer proteins straight from frozen**
3. **fridge-to-mouth protein sides**
4. **frozen emergency foods that become dinner with one sauce**

These are more serious competitors than another 40-minute skillet recipe.

# Branch 1 — Rice-cooker Hainanese chicken rice

This is one of the first meals that genuinely threatens the control.

A Lee Kum Kee rice-cooker version uses roughly:

- jasmine rice
- chicken broth
- boneless chicken thighs
- minced ginger
- minced garlic
- scallion
- salt

Prep is listed at about **10 minutes**, then the rice cooker handles the cooking. The chicken sits over the seasoned rice so its juices season the rice while it cooks.

Marion's Kitchen has a similarly simple one-pot version with roughly **10 minutes prep / 35 minutes cook**.

### Why it is interesting

The attention pattern can be almost as low as wings + rice:

1. put rice/broth/aromatics in cooker
2. put chicken on top
3. press button
4. remove/slice chicken
5. add chili sauce / scallion oil / cucumber if desired

A truly lazy version can skip making fresh scallion oil and use a permanent-condiment-library finisher such as chili crisp, soy, Kewpie, or bottled ginger-scallion sauce.

### Safety rule

Only use this raw-chicken-in-rice-cooker method if the specific rice cooker is suitable for mixed dishes/raw meat. Verify the thickest chicken pieces reach **74°C / 165°F**.

### First test

Use boneless thighs, jarred/frozen ginger + garlic, and one bottled sauce. Keep active prep under 10 minutes.

References:
- Lee Kum Kee rice-cooker Hainanese chicken rice: https://usa.lkk.com/en/recipes/rice-cooker-hainanese-chicken-rice-recipe
- Marion's Kitchen one-pot Hainanese chicken rice: https://www.marionskitchen.com/easy-one-pot-hainanese-chicken-rice/
- Health Canada poultry temperature: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/general-food-safety-tips/safe-internal-cooking-temperatures.html

# Branch 2 — Simplified takikomi gohan

Traditional takikomi gohan cooks rice directly with dashi/soy/mirin plus small amounts of chicken, mushrooms, vegetables, tofu products, or fish.

The classic Just One Cookbook version is too prep-heavy for this project, but the **method** is excellent:

- seasoned liquid goes into the rice cooker
- small ingredients sit on top
- press button
- fluff and eat

### Project version

Strip it down to something like:

- white rice
- dashi powder
- soy
- mirin
- boneless thigh pieces **or cooked/canned fish**
- frozen peas/corn
- pre-sliced mushrooms or dried shiitake

Use one or two add-ins, not eight.

### Important rice-cooker lesson

Just One Cookbook specifically warns against overloading mixed rice: ingredients should stay a minority of the finished rice, and the ingredients are placed on top rather than thoroughly mixed before cooking so the rice hydrates properly.

### Especially interesting fish branch

Salmon + mushroom mixed rice is a natural way to make the twice-weekly fish module feel like dinner rather than nutrition homework.

References:
- Just One Cookbook takikomi gohan: https://www.justonecookbook.com/takikomi-gohan/
- Cooking with Dog salmon/mushroom takikomi gohan: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGHzW3MNq84

# Branch 3 — Lap cheong rice: the absurd emergency control

The Woks of Life's rice-cooker lap cheong recipe is almost offensively simple:

- rice
- water
- Chinese sausage on top
- press button
- slice sausage when done

Their recipe lists about **5 minutes prep / 25 minutes total**.

### Why it deserves a slot

This may be the lowest-attention hot meal found so far. Add arugula/bok choy, chili crisp, garlic, or a cold tofu side and dinner exists.

### Why it should stay a rotation meal

Lap cheong is processed meat and can be high in sodium and saturated fat. Health Canada recommends limiting processed meats as a regular dietary backbone.

So this is a **glorious emergency meal**, not the daily protein strategy.

References:
- The Woks of Life lap cheong rice: https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sausage-rice-cooker-lap-cheong/
- Health Canada highly processed foods / processed meat: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-guide/explore/healthy-eating-recommendations/limit-highly-processed.html

# Branch 4 — Frozen salmon straight into the air fryer

This is perhaps the strongest fish-module discovery.

Multiple air-fryer recipes cook salmon **directly from frozen**, with no thawing. A useful two-stage method is:

1. air-fry frozen salmon briefly to thaw the surface
2. add glaze/seasoning
3. finish cooking

Typical total time is around **15–20 minutes** depending on thickness and fryer.

### Control-level dinner

- start rice cooker
- put frozen salmon in air fryer
- halfway through, brush on teriyaki / miso / maple-Dijon / gochujang glaze
- dump rice + salmon + greens together

That is almost the exact current wing workflow, except the protein is boneless and supplies EPA/DHA.

### Flavour branches

- teriyaki
- miso-honey
- gochujang-honey
- lemon-Dijon
- soy-Kewpie
- pesto-parmesan

### Safety

Health Canada lists **70°C / 158°F** as the safe internal temperature for fish.

References:
- frozen salmon air-fryer method: https://forktospoon.com/how-to-make-frozen-salmon-in-the-air-fryer/
- Health Canada safe cooking temperatures: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/general-food-safety-tips/safe-internal-cooking-temperatures.html

# Branch 5 — Frozen dumplings in the air fryer

The Woks of Life has a particularly interesting method because it is almost supervision-free:

- fresh or frozen dumplings
- oil
- a little water
- air fryer

For frozen dumplings they suggest roughly **12–14 minutes**, and they note that flipping is unnecessary for good overall crispness.

### Why it fits

This is a genuine freezer-to-dinner food with nearly no raw-food handling.

The downside is macro density: dumplings are usually not an efficient primary protein anchor.

### Best role

Use dumplings as the palatable starch/meat component and pair them with:

- skyr tub
- cold tofu
- eggs
- extra chicken
- edamame

This is a perfect "I want something fun but refuse to cook" branch.

Reference:
- The Woks of Life air-fryer dumplings: https://thewoksoflife.com/air-fryer-dumplings-from-fresh-or-frozen/

# Branch 6 — Frozen meatballs as "wings without bones"

Precooked frozen meatballs can go straight into the air fryer and heat in roughly **11–12 minutes** depending on size.

Then one sauce turns them into a totally different meal:

- marinara + parmesan
- teriyaki
- Korean gochujang
- Swedish-ish cream sauce
- BBQ
- curry
- sweet-and-sour

### Why this is interesting

The workflow is extremely close to wings:

1. rice cooker on
2. frozen meatballs in air fryer
3. heat
4. toss in sauce
5. eat

Advantages over wings:

- no bones
- almost 100% edible yield
- very quick eating
- no raw-meat handling if using fully cooked frozen meatballs

The main variable is finding a meatball that actually tastes good and has a reasonable protein/sodium profile.

Reference:
- air-fryer frozen meatball method: https://drdavinahseats.com/frozen-meatballs-in-air-fryer

# Branch 7 — Hiyayakko: the savoury skyr-tub cousin

Cold tofu may be one of the best analogues to the "open skyr tub and eat" philosophy.

Traditional hiyayakko is chilled tofu with simple toppings such as:

- soy sauce
- ginger
- scallion
- bonito flakes

Just One Cookbook describes it as a **no-cook** dish.

### Ultra-lazy project version

Forget the full garnish ceremony:

- open soft/silken tofu
- drain the obvious water
- add ponzu or soy
- add chili crisp
- add furikake / bonito if wanted
- eat

No cutting board required.

### Good pairings

- rice + wings + cold tofu
- rice + dumplings + cold tofu
- rice + canned fish + cold tofu
- rice + salmon + cold tofu

This could add variety and protein without touching the air-fryer/rice-cooker workflow.

Reference:
- Just One Cookbook hiyayakko: https://www.justonecookbook.com/japanese-tofu-cold-tofu-hiyayakko/

# Branch 8 — "rice plus one thing" emergency ladder

Keep a few dinners that require almost no decision-making.

## Level 1: one appliance

- lap cheong rice
- rice-cooker Hainanese chicken
- simplified takikomi gohan

## Level 2: rice cooker + air fryer

- frozen salmon + rice
- frozen meatballs + rice
- frozen dumplings + rice
- boneless chicken thighs + rice

## Level 3: open-and-dump

- hot rice + cold tofu + chili crisp
- hot rice + canned mackerel/sardines + lemon/soy
- hot rice + rotisserie chicken + Kewpie/gochujang
- skyr tub + seeds + honey/berries

The project should deliberately keep a few options in all three levels.

# New idea: attention-event budget

We do not need exact stopwatch measurements every day.

A rough score is enough:

- **0–3 attention events:** legendary convenience
- **4–6:** control-level convenience
- **7–10:** acceptable if especially delicious or batchable
- **10+:** must produce multiple future meals or be a special cooking night

This is probably a better fit for real life than weighing every gram.
