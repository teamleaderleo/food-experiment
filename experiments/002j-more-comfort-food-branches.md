# Experiment 002J: More Comfort-Food Branches

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D menu expansion

## Goal

Expand the meal family by changing **cooking mode** as well as cuisine.

The project should include:

- 20–30 minute skillet / rice-bowl meals
- braises that batch beautifully
- freezer-friendly bakes
- leftover-rescue meals
- soft, saucy foods that remain easy to eat at bulking portions

The same nutrition engine can survive underneath all of them.

---

## Meal 21 — Soboro don

Japanese ground-chicken-and-egg rice bowl.

### Core

- soft white rice
- ground chicken, or finely minced thigh meat
- soy sauce
- mirin
- sake
- ginger
- a little sugar
- softly scrambled eggs
- peas
- pickled ginger

### Why it fits

- ground chicken and tiny egg curds require very little chewing
- rice carries the sweet-savory seasoning
- eggs fit naturally instead of feeling bolted on for choline
- Just One Cookbook's current version is a roughly **30-minute** weeknight meal
- great hot, and also a proven bento format

### Bulk adaptation

Use a larger rice portion, 200–250 g raw-equivalent chicken depending the rest of the day, 2–3 eggs, and a small acidic pickle component. Add a little neutral oil or Kewpie if more calories are needed.

### Main R&D question

Does ground chicken stay juicy enough at a huge serving size, or does pork/beef win on late-meal palatability?

## Meal 22 — Oyakodon

Chicken and egg simmered in a sweet-savory dashi broth over rice.

### Core

- white rice
- boneless chicken thighs, sliced small
- onion
- dashi
- soy sauce
- mirin
- sugar
- 2–3 softly set eggs
- scallion / mitsuba if available

### Why it fits

Just One Cookbook's standard formula is a **one-pan, ~25-minute meal** built from chicken thigh, onion, egg, dashi, soy, mirin and sugar.

For this project it is almost ideal:

- broth keeps everything wet
- egg and onion become extremely soft
- chicken pieces are small
- rice absorbs the sauce
- low cleanup
- easy to scale with more rice

### Bulk adaptation

Keep the chicken portion moderate and let extra rice + sauce carry the calorie increase. A drizzle of sesame/canola oil or a side of Kewpie can raise calories without making the bowl physically huge.

### Palatability target

Silky egg, tender thigh meat, and enough broth that the rice underneath is seasoned all the way down.

## Meal 23 — San bei ji / three-cup chicken rice

Taiwanese/Chinese three-cup chicken lane.

### Core flavour

- chicken thigh or wings
- Shaoxing wine
- soy sauce
- sesame oil
- ginger
- garlic
- dried chili
- sugar
- Thai basil

The Woks of Life version cooks in about **45 minutes total**, with a covered braise followed by reduction and basil at the end.

### Why it fits

- very high flavour intensity
- rice is perfect for the reduced sauce
- chicken thigh makes a juicier daily version than breast
- Thai basil gives a totally different sensory lane from rou zao fan despite overlapping soy/Shaoxing pantry items

### Throughput adaptation

Use boneless thighs cut small instead of wings if chewing/bones are annoying. Keep more braising liquid than a restaurant-style dry reduction if the giant bowl eats better that way.

### Bulk version

Serve with soft white rice, a small blanched green vegetable, and a vinegar pickle. Add an egg only if the day's protein/choline plan calls for it.

## Meal 24 — Jjajangmyeon / jjajangbap

Korean-Chinese black-bean sauce over noodles or rice.

### Core

- pork belly, shoulder, or ground pork
- chunjang / Korean black-bean paste
- onion
- zucchini
- potato or radish
- neutral oil
- cornstarch slurry
- wheat noodles or rice
- cucumber garnish optional

Maangchi's classic method browns pork, stir-fries vegetables, fries the black-bean paste, then simmers the whole sauce and lightly thickens it.

### Why it fits

- thick glossy sauce is ideal for throughput
- pork can be finely chopped or ground
- one batch can serve noodles one day and rice the next
- the flavor family is very different from gochujang, soy-vinegar or mapo

### Bulk adaptation

Cut the vegetables smaller than restaurant chunks if chewing becomes annoying. Use enough sauce that the noodles/rice remain coated. Keep the potato modest because noodles/rice already provide plenty of starch.

### Main R&D question

Noodles versus rice for eating speed and fullness.

## Meal 25 — Filipino chicken adobo rice

### Core

- chicken thighs
- soy sauce
- vinegar
- garlic
- black peppercorn
- bay leaves
- rice

The classic basic pattern is almost comically efficient: marinate, then braise the chicken in the same soy-vinegar-garlic liquid until tender and reduce the liquid into sauce.

### Why it fits

- around 10 minutes active prep in many versions
- unattended braising does the work
- excellent leftovers
- very little specialty pantry expansion once soy/vinegar already exist
- vinegar gives built-in anti-fatigue acidity

### Throughput adaptation

Use boneless thighs or shred bone-in thighs after braising. Keep a generous amount of reduced-but-still-pourable sauce for the rice.

### Rotation advantage

This is one of the cheapest ways to make chicken taste completely different from shawarma, curry or garlic-parmesan.

## Meal 26 — Hayashi rice

Japanese hashed beef / demi-glace rice.

### Core

- thin-sliced beef
- onion
- mushrooms
- butter/oil
- demi-glace / hayashi roux
- tomato
- Worcestershire
- soy
- rice

Just One Cookbook's easy roux version is around **40 minutes total** and uses thin beef, onions and mushrooms simmered into a rich sauce over rice.

### Why it fits

- lets beef feel luxurious without eating a giant steak
- thin beef chews quickly
- velvety sauce soaks into rice
- sauce mix makes the weeknight version much easier than from-scratch demi-glace

### Spend logic

This is exactly where the project should happily pay more for a nice thin-sliced beef cut: moderate quantity, huge flavour/texture payoff.

### Main R&D question

Does this beat gyudon as the preferred beef-rice splurge lane?

## Meal 27 — Cheesy cottage pie

### Core

- ground beef
- onion + garlic
- beef broth / gravy
- frozen peas/carrots or mixed vegetables
- mashed potatoes
- milk
- butter / margarine
- cheddar

Budget Bytes' version explicitly leans on frozen vegetables and creamy cheddar mashed potatoes, which maps perfectly to the convenience thesis.

### Why it fits

- one casserole can create several giant portions
- extremely soft
- mashed potatoes are easy calories
- ground beef stays integrated in gravy
- freezer/reheat friendly
- frozen vegetables make complete sense here

### Bulk adaptation

Use a meatier filling than a conventional pie, enough gravy to stay moist after reheating, and cheese in the mash. Add more milk/oil rather than simply piling on extra potato if volume becomes too high.

### Production mode

**Batch-bake night.** Higher first-night effort, nearly zero effort for several later meals.

## Meal 28 — Egyptian macarona béchamel

### Core

- pasta
- ground beef
- onion
- tomato puree/sauce
- cinnamon/allspice/nutmeg style warm spices
- milk
- flour
- butter/oil
- béchamel
- optional mozzarella

Macarona béchamel is essentially a ground-meat pasta bake covered in creamy béchamel. Current recipe references describe a ground beef/tomato layer with pasta and béchamel baked until browned and bubbling.

### Why it fits

This might be one of the most naturally bulking dishes in the entire project:

- pasta
- meat
- milk-based sauce
- added fat
- optional cheese
- soft casserole texture
- batch/freezer friendliness

### Tradeoff

More active prep than a curry or rice bowl.

Therefore treat it as a **weekend batch luxury**, where 45–60 minutes once buys several almost-effortless meals later.

### R&D adaptation

Hide spinach in the meat layer. Keep the béchamel loose enough that leftovers remain creamy after reheating.

## Meal 29 — Chinese eggplant with garlic sauce + pork

### Core

- Chinese/Japanese eggplant
- small ground-pork component
- garlic
- ginger
- dried chilies
- oyster sauce
- rice vinegar
- Shaoxing wine
- light/dark soy
- sesame oil
- cornstarch
- rice

The Woks of Life's current recipe uses **4 oz / ~115 g pork for a pound of eggplant**, plenty of garlic, and a sweet-savory-tangy sauce. Their updated technique sears the eggplant instead of deep-frying it and keeps the finished dish very saucy.

### Why it fits

- cooked eggplant is extremely soft
- eggplant willingly carries oil and sauce, which is useful during a bulk
- only a modest quantity of pork creates a meat-rich flavour
- the sauce is already designed to spoon over rice
- great leftovers

### Bulk adaptation

Increase pork somewhat if protein is needed, or pair with eggs/tofu. Keep eggplant quantity moderate enough that the bowl remains calorie-dense.

### Interesting role

This is a rare vegetable-forward meal that still fits the project because the vegetable itself becomes silky and calorie-friendly.

# Production-mode rotation

The meal family now covers several different kinds of evening:

| Mode | Best candidates |
| --- | --- |
| ~25–30 minute bowl | oyakodon, soboro don, gochujang pork rice |
| quick noodles | dan dan, peanut-chili noodles |
| unattended braise | rou zao fan, adobo, san bei ji |
| air fryer + rice cooker | shawarma chicken rice |
| sauce-mix comfort | Japanese curry, hayashi rice |
| big weekend batch | cottage pie, macarona béchamel |
| leftover rescue | kimchi fried rice, burrito melts |

This matters because convenience fatigue can happen even when flavour rotates. Some days the best dinner is the one with 8 minutes of attention; other days a casserole is worth making because tomorrow's dinner becomes free.

# Strongest additions from this round

### Tier S candidates

1. **Oyakodon** — perhaps the purest throughput meal discovered so far.
2. **Chicken adobo** — absurdly low ingredient complexity for such a distinct result.
3. **Macarona béchamel** — strongest freezer/batch indulgence lane.
4. **Chinese eggplant + pork** — unusually soft, saucy, vegetable-rich bulk food.

### Tier A candidates

5. Soboro don
6. San bei ji
7. Hayashi rice
8. Jjajangmyeon/jjajangbap
9. Cottage pie
