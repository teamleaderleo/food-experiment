# Experiment 002X: World Tour Round 5 — Rice Kingdoms, Marinade Machines, Soft Tofu + Reusable Bases

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D expansion

## Goal

Keep expanding the food atlas while prioritizing dishes that either:

1. fit the existing low-fuss / phone-compatible workflow almost immediately, or
2. teach a reusable trick that unlocks several future meals.

This round leaned especially hard into:

- chicken + rice civilizations
- braises that mostly cook themselves
- egg / tofu meals that feel substantial without a large meat serving
- reusable aromatic bases
- marinades that belong in a Ziploc
- seafood formats beyond “salmon fillet + rice”

Standing rules still apply:

- chicken / fish / tofu / eggs / dairy first
- pork/beef as flavour-heavy rotation foods
- hand-fuss matters more than elapsed clock time
- buy prepared aromatics when chopping is the thing blocking dinner
- preserve the identity of genuinely special dishes instead of forcing every recipe into fake shortcut food

# 1. Korean sundubu jjigae — huge everyday contender

Sundubu jjigae is spicy Korean soft-tofu stew. Korean Bapsang's kimchi version is explicitly **under 30 minutes** and can be made with meat, seafood, vegetables, or combinations.

Core:

- extra-soft tofu
- kimchi / broth
- gochugaru
- garlic
- sesame oil
- egg
- optional modest meat or seafood

## Why it fits

- tofu carries most of the volume
- egg fits naturally
- extremely soft / low-chew
- one pot
- fast enough for weekday food
- pork is optional rather than foundational
- seafood version gives another fish/shellfish lane

### Project versions

1. **tofu + egg + kimchi** — nearly meatless everyday version
2. **tofu + shrimp + egg** — seafood night
3. **tofu + small amount of ground chicken/pork + egg** — richer version

This may be one of the best ways to make tofu feel like serious comfort food rather than a side.

Sources:
- Korean Bapsang — Sundubu Jjigae: https://www.koreanbapsang.com/kimchi-soondubu-jjigae-soft-tofu-stew-kimchi/
- Maangchi sundubu recipe index: https://www.maangchi.com/recipes/sundubu-jjigae

# 2. Vietnamese caramel ginger chicken — absurd payoff for five ingredients

RecipeTin Eats' Vietnamese caramel ginger chicken is:

- **7 min prep**
- **15 min cook**
- five core ingredients
- only a **12-minute braise**

Core:

- chicken thighs
- fish sauce
- sugar
- ginger
- optional chile / shallot

## Why it is special

It is a genuinely traditional Vietnamese caramel-braising idea rather than generic “Asian sauce.”

The technique turns sugar + fish sauce + ginger into a glossy sweet-savory glaze with extremely little pantry complexity.

### Project version

Use boneless thighs, jarred/frozen ginger, rice cooker rice, and skip fresh garnish unless it is already around.

This is a **normal-cooking meal with control-level ingredient complexity**.

Source: RecipeTin Eats — Vietnamese Caramel Ginger Chicken: https://www.recipetineats.com/vietnamese-caramel-ginger-chicken/

Alternative longer coconut version: https://www.recipetineats.com/vietnamese-coconut-caramel-chicken/

# 3. Filipino chicken inasal — another elite Ziploc chicken lane

Chicken inasal uses a flavour profile built around:

- calamansi / citrus
- vinegar
- lemongrass
- garlic
- annatto / achiote
- chicken oil / basting fat

Panlasang Pinoy emphasizes that the marinade is distinct from soy-based Filipino barbecue: tangy, citrusy, lemongrass-heavy chicken with orange annatto color.

## Why it fits

This is essentially designed for the project:

> chicken in bag → citrus/vinegar/lemongrass marinade → squish → fridge → air fry / grill / broil

Use lemongrass paste and bottled calamansi juice if good versions are available.

### Project simplification

- boneless thighs instead of large bone-in pieces when eating speed wins
- annatto oil / powder rather than making elaborate basting oil from scratch
- rice cooker garlic rice or plain rice
- bottled vinegar dipping sauce if desired

This gives a Filipino chicken night that tastes completely different from adobo.

Sources:
- Panlasang Pinoy — Chicken Inasal: https://panlasangpinoy.com/grilled-chicken-inasal-recipe/
- Bon Appétit — Chicken Inasal: https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/chicken-inasal

# 4. Malaysian nasi lemak — a meal system rather than a single recipe

Nasi lemak is built around fragrant coconut rice plus modular accompaniments.

SBS gives a **5-minute prep / 25-minute cook** coconut rice that works in a rice cooker:

- rice
- coconut milk
- water
- ginger
- pandan
- salt

Then dinner can be assembled from:

- jarred / prepared sambal
- hard-boiled or fried egg
- peanuts
- anchovies if wanted
- cucumber
- leftover / air-fryer chicken

## Why it fits

The rice cooker creates the cuisine identity before the protein even enters the picture.

This creates a new architecture:

> **special rice + refrigerator/pantry toppings + optional easy protein**

That is useful because not every “vacation” has to come from seasoning the chicken.

### Ultra-lazy version

- coconut-pandan rice cooker rice
- bought sambal
- fried eggs
- peanuts
- air-fryer chicken if needed

SBS even has a quick leftover-rice + sambal + fried-egg dish at about **10 min prep / 10 min cook**, reinforcing that sambal itself is a high-value shortcut.

Sources:
- SBS — Coconut Rice / Nasi Lemak: https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/coconut-rice-nasi-lemak/v5v15b6oj
- SBS — Nasi Lemak: https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/nasi-lemak/gw5nqvm6d
- SBS — leftover rice + sambal + eggs: https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/mums-malaysian-sambal-lemak-with-leftover-rice-fried-eggs-and-cucumber/wc9xq4p48

# 5. Somali bariis iskukaris — one-pot spiced chicken + rice

The NHS has a straightforward one-pot bariis iskukaris using:

- chicken
- rice
- onion / garlic
- tomato
- cinnamon
- cumin
- coriander
- turmeric
- carrot

The method is essentially:

1. brief aromatic/spice sauté
2. add chicken
3. simmer covered
4. add rice
5. steam until fluffy

The source explicitly says it **freezes brilliantly**.

## Why it fits

- chicken + rice finish in one pot
- spice profile is genuinely new relative to our Asian / Latin lanes
- very batchable
- phone-compatible after setup
- fresh cilantro is optional

### Project simplification

Use frozen diced onion, garlic paste and pre-shredded carrot or frozen carrot if desired.

Source: NHS Healthier Families — Bariis Iskukaris: https://www.nhs.uk/healthier-families/recipes/bariis-iskukaris/

# 6. Saudi / Arabian kabsa — guest-worthy chicken and rice

Kabsa is a fragrant Arabian Peninsula chicken-and-rice family built around:

- chicken
- basmati rice
- tomato
- cinnamon
- cardamom
- cumin
- turmeric
- dried lime / warm spice
- carrots in some family versions
- nuts + raisins as finishers

Serious Eats' 2026 coverage describes the rice cooking with the chicken juices and spices, then being finished with toasted nuts and jammy raisins.

## Why it fits

This is the fancy end of the same one-pot chicken/rice architecture we already like.

It looks like celebration food while sharing groceries with ordinary chicken-and-rice meals.

### Project rule

Buy a decent **kabsa / baharat-style spice blend** if that keeps the ingredient list sane. Nuts and raisins are optional guest-night finishers rather than a requirement for ordinary dinners.

Source context: Serious Eats — Arab-world chicken roundup / Kabsa: https://www.seriouseats.com/arab-world-chicken-recipes-11979649

# 7. Yemeni chicken mandi — oven architecture worth stealing

Mandi is aromatic Yemeni chicken and rice traditionally associated with a smoky cooking method.

Modern oven versions use a clever setup:

- aromatic liquid / whole spices below
- chicken elevated above
- chicken roasts while its juices season the liquid
- rice goes into the flavoured liquid
- chicken returns above the rice
- optional charcoal smoke finish

Amira's Pantry lists an oven version at about **15 min prep / 1 hr 15 min cook**; Yemeni cooking channels show similar rack-over-rice logic.

## Why it is exciting

This is almost a **guest-mode appliance hack**:

> oven does the chicken + rice flavour transfer automatically

The charcoal step is optional. The dish can still work as a spiced roast-chicken-and-rice system without it.

### Project simplification

- use a pre-mixed **hawaij / mandi spice blend**
- skip blended fresh daqoos unless buying prepared sauce or feeling ambitious
- use chicken thighs if carving whole birds is annoying

Sources:
- Amira's Pantry — Chicken Mandi: https://amiraspantry.com/mandi-recipe/
- Our Yemeni Kitchen oven method: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TM2AbTWaKrc

# 8. Cuban mojo chicken — almost perfect baseline mutation

Mojo chicken is essentially citrus-garlic chicken with oregano/cumin-type seasoning.

The most project-aligned version is almost embarrassingly simple: McCormick's bottled-mojo version is **5 min prep + 45 min roast** and literally uses a resealable bag.

## Why it fits

- Ziploc-native
- bright citrus profile
- chicken-thigh compatible
- rice + black beans + avocado / premade pico all fit naturally
- bottled mojo can replace the blender entirely

### Two modes

**Zero-fuss:** bottled mojo marinade + chicken thighs + air fryer / oven.  
**Better homemade:** orange + lime + garlic + oil + cumin/oregano, mixed directly in the bag.

Sources:
- McCormick — Roasted Orange Mojo Chicken Thighs: https://www.mccormick.com/blogs/lawrys-recipes/roasted-orange-mojo-chicken-thighs

# 9. Haitian epis — reusable aromatic asset, not merely a recipe

Epis is a Haitian seasoning base made from herbs/aromatics such as garlic, parsley/cilantro, scallions, thyme, bell pepper, chile, acid and oil.

Bon Appétit describes it as foundational across Haitian cooking and notes that it can be refrigerated or **frozen in portions**.

This is one of the most important workflow discoveries since Cantonese master sauce.

## Why it fits

Make or buy a batch once, then use spoonfuls for:

- chicken marinades
- Haitian stews
- rice + beans
- soups
- vegetables
- sauces

### Flagship dish: poul ak nwa

Haitian chicken with cashews uses epis and is associated with Sunday / special-occasion cooking. Bon Appétit's recipe intentionally makes extra epis for future dishes.

This creates a new category:

> **fresh aromatic base made rarely → freezer cubes → weeks of shortcut cooking**

Sources:
- Bon Appétit — Epis: https://www.bonappetit.com/story/epis-seasoning
- Bon Appétit — Poul Ak Nwa: https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/poul-ak-nwa-chicken-with-cashews

# 10. Trinidadian curry chicken — curry powder + green seasoning as a system

Trinidadian curry chicken uses two high-leverage assets:

- curry powder / roasted cumin / related spice mix
- **Caribbean green seasoning**

CaribbeanPot has a 20-minute curry-chicken-and-spinach approach when potatoes are pre-cooked; traditional home-style versions braise longer.

## Why it fits

The interesting thing is not chasing an exact 20-minute number. It is learning that **green seasoning can play the same role as epis/sofrito**.

### Project version

- chicken thighs
- bottled or batch-made Caribbean green seasoning
- Trinidad-style curry powder
- baby potatoes / pre-cooked potatoes
- frozen spinach
- rice or bought roti

This should be a very good normal-cooking-pays curry lane.

Sources:
- CaribbeanPot — 20 Minute Curry Chicken with Spinach: https://caribbeanpot.com/20-minute-curry-chicken-with-spinach/
- Canadian Cookbooks — Trinidadian Curry Chicken: https://canadiancookbooks.ca/trinidadian-curry-chicken/

# 11. Thai pad kra pao — active cooking can still win

Hot Thai Kitchen's updated 2026 all-purpose pad kra pao formula is designed to work with essentially **any meat, seafood or leftover cooked protein** and comes together in under 30 minutes.

Classic architecture:

- ground/minced protein
- garlic + chile
- savory sauce
- holy basil
- rice
- fried egg

## Why it survives the project's anti-babysitting bias

This is a stir-fry, so attention density is higher.

But the payoff is:

- extremely short cooking window
- ground chicken works perfectly
- rice cooker runs separately
- fried egg gives extra calories/protein
- one of Thailand's genuine everyday “fast food” formats

This belongs in **normal cooking that pays** rather than unattended cooking.

Source: Hot Thai Kitchen — Pad Kra Pao: https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/

# 12. Korean jjimdak — noodles + chicken + potato braise

Jjimdak is Korean soy-braised chicken with vegetables and sweet-potato glass noodles.

Korean Bapsang gives roughly **20 min prep / 30 min cook**. Maangchi's 2026 update uses chicken, potato, carrot, mushrooms, peppers and glass noodles in a dark sweet-savory sauce.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- soft glass noodles
- potatoes absorb sauce
- braise format
- excellent communal / guest presentation
- can be made ahead through most of the recipe and finished with noodles later

## Friction warning

Traditional vegetable list is long.

### Project simplification

Use:

- boneless thighs
- baby potatoes
- pre-sliced mushrooms
- frozen carrot / onion if acceptable
- skip decorative cucumber / multiple pepper colours

The sauce + chicken + potato + glass noodles are the identity; the vegetable census is negotiable.

Sources:
- Korean Bapsang — Jjimdak: https://www.koreanbapsang.com/jjimdakdakjjim-korean-style-braised/
- Maangchi — Jjimdak: https://www.maangchi.com/recipe/dakjjim

# 13. Filipino arroz caldo — low-chew chicken/rice comfort

Arroz caldo is Filipino chicken rice porridge flavored with ginger, garlic, fish sauce and calamansi/lemon, often topped with egg and fried garlic.

Panlasang Pinoy's current recipe lists **10 min prep / 1 hr cook**.

## Why it is interesting

- extremely soft
- chicken + rice + egg
- warm, easy-to-eat comfort food
- rotisserie-chicken versions exist for convenience
- toppings can come from jars / packets

## Friction warning

The traditional stovetop version asks for occasional stirring so rice does not stick.

This makes it more attended than a rice-cooker meal.

### Future experiment

Test whether a rice cooker / pressure cooker can produce a good enough version with materially less attention.

Sources:
- Panlasang Pinoy — Arroz Caldo: https://panlasangpinoy.com/arroz-caldo-/
- Current 2026 chicken version: https://panlasangpinoy.com/chicken-arroz-caldo-recipe-glutinous-rice-porridge/

# 14. Basque marmitako — fish + potato stew

Marmitako is a Basque tuna-and-potato stew with tomato, peppers, onion, garlic and red-pepper flavour.

Spanish Sabores describes its version as **ready in under an hour**.

## Why it fits

- fish night without salmon
- potato-heavy and calorie friendly
- one-pot stew
- seafood cooks very briefly at the end
- tomato/pepper profile differs completely from coconut fish curries

### Project simplification

Use frozen diced onion/pepper, cherry tomatoes or canned tomato, and frozen tuna steaks if quality is good enough.

This is a strong “normal cooking pays” seafood branch.

Source: Spanish Sabores — Marmitako: https://spanishsabores.com/marmitako-recipe-basque-tuna-stew/

# 15. Sheet-pan musakhan logic — authenticity-preserving shortcut

Serious Eats recently published a weeknight-friendly Palestinian musakhan-inspired chicken-and-potato version.

Traditional musakhan is chicken, softened onions, sumac and bread. Their streamlined version puts chicken, onion, potatoes and spices together on a sheet pan so the potatoes absorb the chicken fat and seasoning.

## Why this is useful

This is exactly the sort of shortcut the project should favor:

- keeps the defining sumac/onion/chicken profile
- simplifies component count
- oven does the work
- potatoes replace hard-to-source taboon bread

This may be a better “real dinner” branch than the earlier ultra-simplified sumac chicken bowl.

Source: Serious Eats — Arab-world chicken roundup / Musakhan: https://www.seriouseats.com/arab-world-chicken-recipes-11979649

# New meta-insight: cuisine can live in the rice or the base

Earlier rounds mostly changed the protein seasoning.

This round reinforced two other powerful levers:

## 1. The starch can carry the destination

- nasi lemak coconut-pandan rice
- kabsa rice
- mandi rice
- Somali bariis
- arroz caldo

The chicken can remain fairly simple while the rice changes the entire experience.

## 2. A reusable aromatic base can replace nightly chopping

- Haitian epis
- Puerto Rican sofrito
- Caribbean green seasoning
- ginger-garlic paste
- Cantonese master sauce after cooking

This suggests a layered pantry/freezer model:

> **dry identity spices + jarred sauces + frozen aromatic bases + one boring protein/starch inventory**

That could support an enormous menu without making any single dinner complicated.
