# Experiment 002U: World Tour Round 2 — Deep Cuts + Workflow Tricks

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D expansion

## Goal

Keep expanding the food universe, but prioritize dishes that either:

1. open a genuinely new flavour lane, or
2. teach a workflow trick that can make future cooking easier.

The standing project rules still apply:

- chicken / fish / tofu / eggs / dairy first
- red meat mostly as a flavour-heavy rotation
- phone-compatible cooking
- low hand-fuss

# 1. Indonesian ayam kecap

A 2026 Woks of Life recipe describes **ayam kecap** as Indonesian chicken braised with kecap manis and aromatics/spices including ginger, garlic, shallot, makrut lime leaf, galangal, coriander and clove.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- braise = phone-compatible
- sweet-savory glaze is very easy to eat with rice
- tastes completely different from adobo / teriyaki / gochujang
- leftovers should reheat well

## Project simplification

Use:

- boneless chicken thighs
- kecap manis
- frozen diced onion / shallot
- ginger + garlic paste
- dried/frozen makrut lime leaf if available
- ground coriander + clove
- rice

Fresh galangal is optional if it becomes a friction point.

### Likely workflow

1. brown chicken
2. dump in aromatics + kecap manis + water
3. cover and braise
4. uncover briefly to glaze
5. serve over rice

This looks like an excellent **normal-cooking-pays** meal.

**Reference:** The Woks of Life — Ayam Kecap, Indonesian Braised Chicken, posted May 6 2026.

# 2. Cantonese soy-sauce chicken + the master-sauce trick

Traditional Cantonese soy-sauce chicken is delicious but a bit involved when done with a whole chicken.

The much more interesting project lesson is the **master sauce**:

> save the soy/aromatic braising liquid, strain it, freeze it, and reuse it for later chicken batches.

The Woks of Life specifically notes that the reserved liquid can be reused several times.

They also have an Instant Pot version using boneless, skinless chicken thighs where the chicken becomes tender and can be shredded over rice.

## Why this matters

This is not just a recipe. It is a reusable flavour asset.

A future dinner can become:

- thaw master sauce
- add chicken
- pressure cook / braise
- rice cooker
- greens

The sauce gets richer over time while reducing measuring and pantry decisions.

## Project version

If a pressure cooker / Instant Pot is already available or ever becomes worth owning, this should be one of the first experiments.

Otherwise, use a stovetop braise with thigh pieces rather than a whole chicken.

### Strong sides

- plain rice
- ginger-scallion oil
- bok choy / arugula
- cold tofu

**References:**
- The Woks of Life — Soy Sauce Chicken, updated Jan 29 2026
- The Woks of Life — Instant Pot Soy Sauce Chicken Bowls

# 3. Puerto Rican arroz con pollo

Budget Bytes' Puerto Rican arroz con pollo is a one-pot chicken-and-rice dish with about **10 minutes hands-on prep / 40 minutes cooking**.

## Why it fits

- one pot
- rice and chicken together
- sofrito gives a full flavour identity
- phone-compatible after initial setup
- leftovers are obvious
- Latin flavour without requiring a pile of fresh chopping

## Project simplification

Use:

- prepared sofrito
- boneless thighs
- frozen pepper/onion blend if needed
- canned tomato
- olives
- rice

This is one of the best arguments for **buying sofrito rather than chopping the aromatics yourself**.

**Reference:** Budget Bytes — Arroz Con Pollo (Puerto Rican Chicken and Rice), prep 10 min / cook 40 min.

# 4. Puerto Rican pollo guisado

Pollo guisado is a Puerto Rican chicken stew with potatoes, carrots, olives and tomato/sofrito-based broth.

Budget Bytes lists the dish at about **15 minutes prep** and under an hour total.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- braise / stew
- excellent leftovers
- sofrito does most of the aromatic work
- potatoes carry calories and thicken the meal naturally
- olives add acid/salt contrast

## Project simplification

Use:

- boneless thighs if bones are annoying
- prepared sofrito
- baby potatoes or pre-cut potatoes
- frozen carrot coins if acceptable

This gives a Caribbean/Latin stew lane that feels very different from curry or adobo.

**Reference:** Budget Bytes — Pollo Guisado.

# 5. Jamaican jerk chicken

Jerk opens a flavour lane the project barely touches: allspice, thyme, chili, ginger, garlic, sweet spice and smoke/char.

Budget Bytes has two useful clues:

### Slow-cooker jerk chicken

- ~15 minutes prep
- several hours unattended
- broil briefly at the end for char

### Jerk chicken + pineapple/black-bean salsa

- ~5 minutes prep in their simplified version using premixed jerk seasoning
- works hot or cold as leftovers

## Project version

The easiest route is probably:

- chicken thighs in Ziploc
- bottled jerk marinade or good jerk seasoning + oil/lime
- squish
- air fry / roast
- rice
- premade pineapple salsa or canned pineapple + jarred salsa if desired

### Why it fits

This is another huge win for the **same chicken-thigh workflow, totally different vacation** idea.

**References:** Budget Bytes — Slow Cooker Jerk Chicken; Jerk Chicken with Pineapple Black Bean Salsa.

# 6. Persian fesenjan

Fesenjan is a Persian chicken stew in a walnut + pomegranate molasses sauce.

The Mediterranean Dish's version is a true special-occasion dish:

- ground walnuts
- chicken thighs
- pomegranate molasses
- turmeric / cinnamon / saffron
- long simmer
- about **2 hr 20 min total**

## Why it is fascinating

- wildly distinctive sweet-sour-nutty flavour
- walnuts make it calorie-dense
- chicken-first
- very guest-worthy
- long cook is mostly simmering
- made for rice

## Why it is not daily food

- food processor / grinding walnuts
- specialty pomegranate molasses
- longer setup
- more expensive ingredients

This belongs in the **guest-night / curiosity / weekend project** tier.

It is exactly the kind of dish that expands the user's food universe rather than merely offering another seasoning blend.

**Reference:** The Mediterranean Dish — Fesenjan, Persian Pomegranate and Walnut Chicken Stew.

# 7. French poulet rôti with potatoes

RecipeTin Eats published a French roast chicken in May 2026 using:

- whole chicken
- Herbes de Provence
- garlic
- butter + olive oil
- potatoes underneath
- pot-roasting rather than a dry tray

**Reference:** RecipeTin Eats — Poulet Rôti, published May 1 2026 / updated Aug 22 2026.

# 9. Malaysian laksa — two tiers

Laksa is spicy coconut noodle soup from Malaysia/Singapore/Indonesia with laksa paste, coconut milk, noodles and proteins such as chicken, shrimp, tofu puffs and fish cake.

## Full restaurant-ish version

RecipeTin Eats uses a store-bought laksa paste "spruced up" to make restaurant-quality broth. The full version is richer and more involved but still avoids making curry paste from scratch.

## Emergency 10-minute version

The Woks of Life published a **10-minute laksa instant ramen** in 2025 using:

- laksa paste
- coconut milk
- instant noodles
- brown sugar
- optional herbs/chili/lime

## Project version

Upgrade the 10-minute version with:

- leftover chicken
- shrimp
- tofu puffs
- egg
- frozen spinach

### Why it fits

This is a great example of turning "college food" into something that feels like a real flavour trip with one jar of paste.

### Rotation note

Coconut milk can push saturated fat up, so laksa is better as a rotation meal than the everyday backbone.

**References:**
- RecipeTin Eats — Laksa Noodle Soup
- The Woks of Life — Laksa Instant Ramen, 10 min

# 10. Sumac chicken + bought pickled onions

A Mediterranean Dish grilled sumac chicken-thigh recipe is inspired by Palestinian musakhan but simplified dramatically:

- chicken thighs
- lemon
- sumac
- warm spices
- pickled onions
- tzatziki / pita
- **10 min prep / 10 min cooking** after marination

The source explicitly notes that store-bought pickled onions can be used.

## Why it fits

This gives some of the musakhan flavour family without caramelizing six onions for forty minutes.

### Project version

- chicken thighs
- sumac marinade in Ziploc
- air fryer / grill / broiler
- store-bought pickled onions
- tzatziki
- pita or rice

This is a very strong **air-fryer vacation** candidate.

**Reference:** The Mediterranean Dish — Grilled Chicken Thighs with Sumac, Pickled Onions, and Charred Lemon.

# 11. Chicken nanban — delicious, but friction warning

Chicken nanban is Japanese fried chicken soaked in a sweet-tangy vinegar sauce and topped with tartar sauce.

A 2026 Just One Cookbook version takes about **50 minutes** and involves:

- butterflying/pounding chicken
- flour + egg coating
- double frying
- making nanban sauce
- chopping onion/cucumber/egg for tartar sauce

## Why it is worth knowing

It looks and tastes fantastic, and leftovers keep well.

## Why it is currently demoted

It hits too many of the user's friction triggers:

- breading station
- deep frying
- multiple bowls
- fine chopping
- repeated hand work

Do not let "interesting world food" turn into a chore.

A future air-fryer shortcut version could be tested if a convincing method appears.

**Reference:** Just One Cookbook — Chicken Nanban, republished Mar 28 2026.

# New meta-insight: reusable flavour assets

The condiment shelf was step one.

The next level is preserving things that have already been cooked:

- master soy braising sauce
- curry base
- cooked salsa chicken
- shawarma-marinated raw chicken bags
- cooked rou zao / ragù
- premixed dan-dan sauce
- portioned pesto / chimichurri

This turns future cooking from "start a recipe" into:

> **pull one flavour asset + one protein/starch → assemble dinner.**

That may be the next major evolution of the project.
