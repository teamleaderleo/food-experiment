# Experiment 002H: Recipe Lab Notes

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D menu expansion

## Goal

Turn the Ultimate Meal project into a rotating set of meals that are:

- genuinely craveable
- easy to eat quickly
- easy to digest relative to their calorie load
- compatible with ~3,000–3,200 kcal/day bulk targets
- compatible with ~130–145 g protein/day
- around ~30–40 g fibre/day across the full day
- low active prep
- easy to batch/reheat
- usually in the ~$10–15/day home-food zone, with room to spend more when it meaningfully improves the meal

The guiding idea is **cheap backbone, premium surface**. Save on starches, milk, eggs, frozen vegetables, pantry staples and bulk proteins; spend on the flavour identity, good meat, cheese, sauces, herbs and convenience.

## Recipe design rules learned so far

### 1. Sauce is a throughput ingredient

Dry food slows eating and becomes tiring late in a large meal.

Prefer:

- loose curry
- glossy pasta sauce
- braising liquid
- minced meat sauce
- silky tofu sauce
- creamy yogurt/tahini sauces
- enough rice/pasta water that starch stays lubricated

A meal should remain moist through the final third.

### 2. Ground / braised / shredded proteins usually beat big chunks

For this project, the best meat texture is often:

- ground pork/beef/chicken
- very finely shredded chicken thigh
- braised pork
- soft tofu + small minced-meat component

This improves eating speed and sauce integration.

### 3. Vegetables should often disappear into the meal

Use small amounts of high-value produce rather than giant raw salads:

- spinach folded into sauce
- peas in curry/pasta
- red pepper finely diced or blended
- tomato concentrate
- cabbage softened into fillings
- frozen onion/pepper blends

Keep one fresh acidic/crisp component when it improves enjoyment.

### 4. Keep a deliberate acid source

Large rich meals become tiring without acid.

Useful low-effort acids:

- rice vinegar
- lime/lemon
- salsa
- pickles
- kimchi
- tomato
- yogurt/skyr sauces

### 5. Use eggs as a flexible micro-module

Two eggs are valuable for choline and can be moved between meals:

- soft-boiled over curry/rice
- soy-marinated egg
- folded into fried rice
- stirred carefully into pasta sauce
- served beside noodles

### 6. Fish should feel like a recipe, not homework

Two fish meals per week can handle EPA/DHA. Build them around dishes where fish belongs naturally:

- salmon teriyaki rice
- mackerel tomato pasta
- sardine puttanesca
- salmon curry
- tuna/salmon mayo rice bowl

### 7. Calorie sliders should be pleasurable

If a meal needs another ~200–400 kcal, prefer something that feels like an upgrade:

- naan with butter/margarine
- toast + jam/honey
- extra rice with sauce
- extra pasta with cheese
- Kewpie/tahini/toum
- milkshake
- cereal + milk

Maltodextrin remains a performance tool for days when chewing/volume becomes the actual bottleneck.

# Second-wave recipe slate

## Meal 6 — Taiwanese lu rou fan bulk bowl

This may be one of the strongest overall candidates.

### Core

- soft white rice
- ground pork or finely chopped pork shoulder
- soy sauce
- dark soy optional
- rice wine / cooking wine if desired
- sugar
- five-spice
- fried shallot/onion or frozen diced onion
- mushrooms optional
- 2 braised or soft-boiled eggs
- small spinach/bok choy portion
- pickled mustard greens or quick-pickled cabbage for acid

### Why it fits

- braised minced pork is soft and extremely easy to eat
- fat is naturally distributed through the sauce
- rice absorbs the braising liquid
- batches/reheats beautifully
- eggs belong naturally
- greens can stay tiny
- strong personal/cultural lane worth exploring

### Bulk version

Use leaner pork plus a little canola oil if the normal pork gets too saturated-fat-heavy. Add tofu cubes if desired, but the dish does not need a soy extender to be economical.

### Palatability target

Sweet-savoury, glossy, deeply soy-braised, with enough pickled acid that the bowl stays exciting.

## Meal 7 — Dan dan noodles, high-throughput edition

### Core

- wheat noodles
- ground pork or beef
- tahini or Chinese sesame paste
- chili oil / chili crisp
- soy sauce
- rice vinegar
- doubanjiang optional
- small sugar amount
- finely chopped spinach/greens
- scallion when available
- crushed peanuts optional

### Why it fits

- sauce is calorie-dense and intensely flavoured
- ground meat requires almost no chewing
- sesame paste supplies calories in very little volume
- noodles are soft and easy to eat
- greens can disappear into the sauce

### R&D variables

- tahini vs Chinese sesame paste vs peanut butter
- pork vs beef
- 0 vs 20–25 g soy granules blended into the mince
- chili crisp quantity versus added neutral oil

### Palatability target

Creamy, nutty, hot, numbing, salty, acidic. Never dry.

## Meal 8 — Gyudon with egg

### Core

- soft white rice
- very thin sliced beef
- onion
- soy sauce
- mirin/sugar
- dashi or bouillon
- 2 eggs: soft-boiled, poached, or onsen-style
- spinach/greens in a small side portion
- pickled ginger or rice-vinegar vegetables

### Why it fits

- thin beef eats much quicker than steak
- braising broth keeps everything soft
- rice is saturated with savoury liquid
- eggs and beef make it feel luxurious
- good use of a modest beef splurge without needing 300 g of steak

### Cost logic

This is exactly where paying more for beef can be worth it because the amount required for flavour/texture can stay moderate.

## Meal 9 — Gochujang pork rice + Kewpie egg

### Core

- rice
- ground pork, beef, chicken, or tofu
- gochujang
- soy sauce
- rice vinegar
- a little sugar/honey
- spinach
- peas or finely chopped red pepper
- 2 fried/soft eggs
- Kewpie
- optional kimchi

### Method idea

Brown meat, stir in gochujang/soy/vinegar/sugar with water to make a glossy sauce, fold in greens, spoon over rice, top with egg and a thin Kewpie zigzag.

### Why it fits

Almost zero prep once the condiment library exists. The sauce is strong enough to make freezer ingredients taste intentional.

## Meal 10 — Shawarma chicken rice with toum-tahini

### Core

- rice or soft naan
- boneless chicken thighs, chopped small or shredded
- shawarma spice blend
- tahini
- toum / garlic sauce
- lemon
- small tomato/cucumber/pepper portion or pickles
- optional yogurt/skyr

### Why it fits

- thigh meat stays juicy
- tahini/toum provide high calorie density and massive flavour
- rice and naan both work as calorie sliders
- pickles/lemon protect against palate fatigue
- extremely close to restaurant comfort food while still easy to control nutritionally

### Convenience version

Use rotisserie chicken + shawarma spice + warmed rice + jarred pickles + tahini/toum.

## Meal 12 — Thai coconut chicken curry

### Core

- rice
- chicken thigh or tofu
- Aroy-D red/green curry paste
- coconut milk, perhaps half-can plus broth/milk
- peas/spinach/red pepper
- lime

### Why it fits

Coconut curry is naturally soft, saucy and calorie-dense. Lime makes a large serving easier to finish.

# Strongest next tests

The most interesting next recipes to actually cook are:

1. **Taiwanese lu rou fan** — huge potential for batchability, softness and flavour.
2. **Dan dan noodles** — best noodle/sesame/chili lane.
3. **Shawarma chicken rice** — restaurant-style comfort with very little specialized prep.
4. **Gyudon** — use beef as a deliberate flavour splurge.
5. **Butter-chicken-ish curry** — excellent mainstream comfort-food test.
6. **Burrito melt** — returns to the original bean/tortilla idea in a more luxurious form.

## Rotation principle

A useful week should cross flavour families:

- one tomato/Italian meal
- one Chinese/Taiwanese meal
- one Japanese meal
- one South-Asian curry
- one Middle-Eastern meal
- one Mexican-ish meal
- one free-choice repeat/fish/splurge

This gives enough sensory distance that the shared grocery backbone can stay repetitive without the dinners feeling repetitive.
