# Experiment 002Q: White-Meat / Fish / Tofu First

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D direction

## Goal

Keep the meal project aligned with the user's preference for feeling better when red meat makes up a smaller share of the diet.

This is not a ban on pork or beef. The new default is:

> **chicken + fish + tofu + eggs + dairy as the everyday backbone; pork/beef as high-value flavour ingredients and rotation meals.**

The project should preserve the rich, comforting meals we liked while reducing the need for large daily red-meat portions.

## Main finding

This shift does not make the menu worse. Several of the strongest low-friction meals are already naturally chicken/fish/tofu based.

---

## 1. Soboro don becomes a serious daily contender

Just One Cookbook's current soboro don uses:

- ground chicken
- ginger
- soy sauce
- mirin
- sake
- a little sugar
- scrambled egg
- peas
- rice

It is listed at about **30 minutes total**, and the chicken can also be made from finely chopped thigh meat.

The useful storage detail: the cooked soboro can stay refrigerated for about **3–4 days** or frozen for roughly a month.

### Why it fits

- ground chicken = almost no chewing
- eggs fit naturally
- rice cooker handles the starch
- sweet-savory profile is easy to eat in a large portion
- topping can be batch-cooked, then reheated over fresh rice
- ground chicken keeps the red-meat share low without feeling like a substitute

### Project adaptation

Use ground thigh meat or a thigh/breast blend if lean ground chicken feels dry. A small drizzle of neutral/sesame oil, Kewpie, or extra sauce is preferable to using pork purely for juiciness.

Reference: Just One Cookbook — Soboro Don.

## 2. Tofu + egg don is an unexpectedly strong no-red-meat lane

Just One Cookbook published a **15-minute** simmered tofu and egg rice bowl in 2026.

The structure is essentially vegetarian oyakodon:

- atsuage / fried firm tofu
- onion
- dashi
- soy
- mirin
- sugar
- eggs
- rice

The tofu simmers in broth, the eggs stay glossy, and the whole thing gets spooned over rice.

### Why it fits

- one pan
- very soft
- very little knife work
- no raw-meat handling
- tofu + egg gives a substantial protein contribution
- rice carries calories
- broth keeps eating throughput high

This may be the best **"I want a hot dinner but don't feel like meat"** meal found so far.

Reference: Just One Cookbook — Simmered Tofu and Egg Rice Bowl.

## 3. Mapo tofu can become tofu-dominant rather than pork-dominant

A current Japanese-style easy mapo recipe uses only about **4 oz / 113 g ground pork for two servings** and explicitly allows another ground meat.

That reinforces the project version:

- silken tofu is the bulk of the protein/food volume
- ground chicken can replace pork entirely when desired
- pork can remain as a smaller flavour dose on some days

### Three useful versions

1. **tofu + 75–100 g pork** — richer red-meat rotation version
2. **tofu + ground chicken** — everyday version
3. **tofu + mushrooms / extra egg** — lower-meat experiment

The sauce identity still comes from doubanjiang/miso/oyster sauce/soy/sesame oil more than from the exact meat.

Reference: Just One Cookbook — Easy Mapo Tofu.

## 4. Crock-pot salsa chicken is almost offensively convenient

Budget Bytes' salsa chicken is:

- chicken breast
- salsa
- chili powder
- garlic powder
- onion powder

Prep is listed at about **10 minutes**, then the slow cooker runs unattended for hours.

### Why it is useful

One batch becomes several meals without tasting like "meal prep":

- chicken rice bowl
- burrito
- quesadilla
- taco
- enchilada filling
- nachos
- baked potato topping

This is exactly the kind of batch food that justifies normal cooking because it creates a fridge ingredient rather than a finished identical meal.

### Project upgrade

Use thighs or a breast/thigh mix if breast alone feels dry. Add chipotle, lime, or adobo sauce when more flavour is worth it.

Reference: Budget Bytes — Crock Pot Salsa Chicken.

## 5. One-pot chicken + rice deserves more attention

RecipeTin Eats has several chicken-and-rice formats where the final cooking is mostly hands-off.

### Chinese-style chicken and rice

- quick chicken marinade
- uncooked rice
- aromatics
- vegetables
- one pot
- roughly **10 min prep / 20 min cook**

### Mexican chicken and rice

The oven-finished version is especially interesting because the author explicitly notes that once it goes in the oven there is **no stirring or watching**.

### Project simplification

Use:

- frozen onion/pepper blend
- garlic/ginger paste
- frozen corn/peas
- bottled salsa / seasoning blend

The original recipes can be made less fussy while preserving the one-pot logic.

References: RecipeTin Eats — One Pot Chinese Chicken and Rice; One Pot Mexican Chicken and Rice.

## 6. Miso salmon is a perfect Ziploc-marination recipe

Just One Cookbook's miso salmon uses:

- salmon
- miso
- sake
- mirin
- soy
- tiny amount sesame oil

It has about **5 minutes active prep + 15 minutes cooking**, after an unattended 1–2 hour marinade.

Leftovers can be refrigerated for about **3 days** or frozen for around a month.

### Project version

Put salmon + marinade ingredients in a resealable bag, squish from outside, refrigerate, then air-fry or broil.

Serve with:

- rice
- ginger rice
- arugula/spinach
- pickles

This is probably the best deliberate fish-night recipe found so far because it fits the user's preferred workflow almost perfectly.

Reference: Just One Cookbook — Miso Salmon.

## 7. Teriyaki salmon: excellent taste, slightly lower phone compatibility

The classic pan version can be done in about **15 minutes**, but it requires searing, steaming, sauce reduction, and a bit more stove attention.

For this project, use the flavour profile while keeping the **air-fryer-from-frozen** method discovered earlier whenever convenience wins.

This gives two salmon modes:

- **air-fryer frozen + bottled/home glaze** for ordinary nights
- **pan teriyaki** when 15 minutes of cooking attention feels worth it

Reference: Just One Cookbook — Teriyaki Salmon.

## 8. One-pot lemon-garlic shrimp + rice

Budget Bytes has a one-pot shrimp-and-rice recipe at roughly **10 minutes prep / 20 minutes cooking**.

### Why shrimp is interesting

- seafood variety beyond salmon/sardines
- extremely quick cooking
- boneless / no eating friction
- frozen shrimp stores well
- works with rice, pasta, curry, tacos

### Downside

Shrimp can be pricier per calorie and is lean, so it needs rice/oil/sauce to serve the bulking mission.

Use as a variety/splurge protein rather than the cheapest daily backbone.

Reference: Budget Bytes — Lemon Garlic Shrimp and Rice.

---

## 9. Tofu curry is a strong leftover food

Budget Bytes' tofu-spinach-pea curry uses tofu, spinach, peas, aromatics/spices and coconut milk and is explicitly described as tasting even better as leftovers.

### Why it fits

- no red meat
- sauce-heavy
- very reheat-friendly
- easy to bulk with rice/naan
- tofu + peas + dairy/soy side can cover protein

### Project tweak

Keep coconut milk moderate if the day already has lots of saturated fat, and add rice/naan/oil for the calorie slider rather than making the curry itself excessively rich.

Reference: Budget Bytes — Tofu, Spinach, and Green Pea Curry.

## 10. Soy-glazed eggplant don + skyr/tofu side

Just One Cookbook's soy-glazed eggplant don is a **20-minute, one-pan** rice bowl.

The eggplant is soft, oily and sauce-friendly, so it is unusually compatible with bulking.

### Protein problem

On its own it is not a strong enough protein anchor for the project.

### Solution

Pair it with one of the direct-eating protein modules:

- skyr tub + seeds
- cold tofu
- eggs
- leftover chicken

This is a good example of **not every main dish needing to carry the entire protein target by itself**.

Reference: Just One Cookbook — Soy-Glazed Eggplant Donburi.

# New protein hierarchy

## Everyday backbone

### Tier S

- chicken thighs
- ground chicken / minced thigh
- salmon / mackerel rotation
- tofu
- eggs
- skyr / yogurt / milk

### Tier A

- chicken breast when it works culinarily
- shrimp
- canned tuna/salmon/mackerel
- edamame
- modest legumes

## Flavour-heavy rotation

- ground pork
- beef

Use them where they buy a lot of enjoyment per gram:

- 75–125 g pork in mapo
- modest pork topping in dan dan
- a real rou zao fan night when desired
- thin beef in gyudon/hayashi

This approach avoids turning every meal into chicken breast while still reducing the overall red-meat proportion.

# Most promising A/B tests

1. **Mapo:** 100 g pork vs ground chicken
2. **Soboro:** ground chicken breast vs ground thigh / thigh blend
3. **Salmon:** miso bag marinade vs bottled teriyaki glaze
4. **Taco/burrito lane:** salsa chicken breast vs chicken thigh
5. **Meatless hot meal:** tofu-egg don vs oyakodon
6. **Protein modularity:** vegetable-heavy main + skyr tub versus forcing more meat into the main

## Current thesis

The project does not need red meat to achieve rich, craveable food.

A better long-run pattern may be:

> **white meat / fish / tofu carry the volume; red meat shows up when its flavour is genuinely worth it.**

That aligns better with comfort, variety, and the existing wings/skyr habits while preserving the best pork/beef recipes as actual treats instead of defaults.
