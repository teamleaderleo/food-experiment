# Experiment 002V: World Tour Round 3 — Fish Stews, Coconut Curries, Egg Food + More

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D expansion

## Goal

Keep broadening the food universe while staying faithful to the real operating constraints:

- chicken / fish / tofu / eggs / dairy first
- red meat mostly as a flavour-heavy rotation
- phone-compatible cooking
- low hand-fuss
- prepared aromatics and spice blends are legitimate tools
- normal cooking is welcome when the payoff or leftovers justify it
- guest-worthy dishes are a bonus

This round deliberately looked for cuisines and dish families that are still underrepresented in the project.

---

# 1. Brazilian moqueca — huge fish-night contender

A current Brazilian-style moqueca recipe uses white fish, coconut milk, tomato and bell pepper and comes together in one pot in roughly **25–30 minutes**.

## Why it fits

- fish-first
- soft / low-chew
- one pot
- vivid color and guest appeal
- rice naturally completes the meal
- coconut + tomato + pepper feels completely different from miso salmon or Mediterranean fish

## Project simplification

Use:

- frozen white-fish portions
- canned coconut milk
- frozen pepper/onion blend
- canned tomato or good fresh tomato when wanted
- lime
- cilantro only when buying herbs sounds fun

Traditional Brazilian versions may use dendê / red palm oil; an easy olive-oil version is still useful as an entry point. If the dish earns repeat status, test a more traditional Bahia-style branch later.

**Reference:** Primavera Kitchen — Brazilian Fish Stew / Moqueca, updated Jul 24 2026.  
https://www.primaverakitchen.com/brazilian-fish-stew/

# 2. Brazilian galinhada — another one-pot chicken-rice country

Galinhada is Brazilian chicken and rice cooked together in one pot, often with turmeric/saffron-like color, tomato, pepper and peas.

A straightforward version is ready within about an hour including marination.

## Why it fits

- same chicken + rice backbone as the control
- one pot
- leftovers
- no need for separate starch management
- prepared Brazilian-style sofrito or frozen onion/pepper can reduce chopping

## Project version

- boneless thighs
- prepared sofrito or frozen onion + garlic paste
- turmeric
- tomato paste
- frozen peas
- rice
- lime / parsley optional

This belongs beside Puerto Rican arroz con pollo and Hainanese chicken rice in the **one-pot chicken-and-rice family**.

**Reference:** I Heart Brazil — Brazilian Chicken and Rice / Galinhada.  
https://www.iheartbrazil.com/brazilian-chicken-and-rice-galinhada-recipe/

# 3. Senegalese chicken yassa — acid + onion + mustard braise

Chicken yassa is a Senegalese chicken dish built around a tart marinade of lemon/citrus, vinegar, mustard, chile and a large quantity of onions.

Food Network versions describe it as an easy one-pot braise after marination; active time is around **35 minutes**, with the long clock time mostly marinating.

## Why it is interesting

- chicken-first
- strong lemon/mustard identity
- onion becomes the sauce rather than garnish
- rice-friendly
- dinner-party capable
- marinade becomes part of the finished sauce

## Friction warning

Traditional yassa uses a lot of onions. Hand-slicing a mountain of onions is exactly the sort of work the project dislikes.

## Project adaptation

Use pre-sliced onions, frozen sliced onions, or a food-processor / mandoline only if already convenient.

Marinate chicken + onions + lemon + Dijon + vinegar in a large Ziploc bag, then braise everything in one pot.

This could be a **normal-cooking-pays** dish rather than a weekly emergency meal.

**References:**
- Food Network — JJ Johnson Chicken Yassa: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/chicken-yassa-9545440
- Food Network — Kardea Brown Senegalese Yassa Poulet: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/senegalese-yassa-17648867

# 4. Ethiopian misir wat — berbere turns lentils into a real flavour destination

Misir wat / misir wot is an Ethiopian red-lentil stew built around onion, berbere, garlic/ginger and often niter kibbeh or ghee.

Several pressure-cooker / simplified versions are around **30 minutes** and one pot.

## Why it fits

- red lentils break down into a soft, spoonable texture
- excellent batch food
- cheap without feeling like cost-optimization food
- berbere creates a completely new flavour family
- can pair with rice, naan/pita, eggs, chicken, yogurt, or injera when available

## Project version

- frozen diced onion
- garlic + ginger paste
- bought berbere
- red lentils
- tomato paste
- ghee / butter / oil

If an Instant Pot enters the kitchen, this is an obvious first pressure-cooker test.

**Reference:** Spice Cravings — Misir Wot, Ethiopian Red Lentils, ~30 min.  
https://spicecravings.com/ethiopian-red-lentil-stew-misir-wot

# 5. Ethiopian doro wat — real weekend project, not fake convenience

Traditional doro wat is a deeply spiced chicken stew with berbere, onions, niter kibbeh and hard-boiled eggs.

Authentic versions often require **large quantities of onions cooked down slowly** and total time around 1.5–2 hours or more.

## Why it still belongs in the atlas

- highly distinctive
- chicken + egg
- excellent with injera or rice
- batch-friendly
- true special-occasion / guest food

## Why it is not a weekday shortcut

The onion base is the dish. Trying to remove that step entirely would remove a major part of the identity.

The project can still reduce friction through:

- pre-chopped onions
- bought berbere
- bought niter kibbeh or ghee
- boneless thighs if bones become annoying

This is a good example of **normal cooking that is worth respecting rather than flattening into a 15-minute hack**.

**Reference:** All About Ethiopia — Doro Wat Recipe, 2026.  
https://www.allaboutethiopia.com/2026/08/doro-wat-recipe.html

# 6. Sri Lankan chicken curry — roasted curry powder is an identity ingredient

Sri Lankan chicken curry uses chicken, roasted curry powder, turmeric, ginger/garlic, curry leaves and often coconut milk, lemongrass or pandan.

A current Guardian guide notes that a good store-bought roasted curry powder is a valid convenience route even though homemade blends offer more control.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- sauce-heavy
- strong leftovers
- rice-friendly
- coconut + roasted spice profile is distinct from butter chicken / Thai curry

## Project simplification

- boneless thighs
- bought Sri Lankan roasted curry powder
- garlic/ginger paste
- frozen onion
- canned coconut milk
- frozen curry leaves if available

Pandan / lemongrass can become optional identity upgrades rather than blockers.

**References:**
- The Guardian — How to make the perfect Sri Lankan chicken curry, Sep 6 2026: https://www.theguardian.com/food/2026/sep/06/how-to-make-perfect-sri-lankan-chicken-curry-recipe-felicity-cloake
- PBS Food — Sri Lankan Chicken Curry: https://www.pbs.org/food/recipes/sri-lankan-chicken-curry

# 7. Kerala fish curry — another serious fish rotation

Kerala-style coconut fish curry can be a **30-minute** meal using white fish, coconut milk, tomato, mild spices and tamarind / kudampuli.

## Why it fits

- fish-first
- one pot
- very soft eating texture
- sauce makes rice easy to eat in quantity
- curry leaves + tamarind create a flavour identity we do not already have

## Project simplification

Use frozen white fish, canned coconut milk, ginger/garlic paste and a bought South-Indian spice blend if helpful.

Curry leaves freeze well enough for cooking, so buying one packet can unlock several future curries without forcing weekly fresh-herb shopping.

**Reference:** The Real Meal Deal — Keralan Fish Curry, updated Jan 13 2026, ~30 min.  
https://www.therealmealdeal.com/keralan-fish-curry/

# 8. Burmese ohn no khao swè — coconut chicken noodles

Ohn no khao swè is Burmese coconut chicken noodle soup, commonly thickened with chickpea flour and topped with egg, lime, herbs, chili oil or crunchy noodles.

An SBS version is listed at roughly **10 minutes prep + 30 minutes cooking**.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- coconut broth
- noodles
- low-chew / high-throughput eating
- toppings are modular rather than mandatory
- visually interesting enough for guests

## Project version

Core bowl:

- chicken thighs
- coconut milk
- turmeric
- chicken stock
- besan / chickpea flour
- noodles
- fish sauce

Lazy toppings:

- boiled egg
- bottled chili oil
- fried shallots from a jar
- lime

Fresh cilantro is optional.

This is an especially good example of a dish that feels elaborate because of the **topping architecture**, even if several toppings come straight from jars.

**Reference:** SBS Food — Coconut Chicken Noodle Soup / Ohn No Khao Swè, 10 min prep / 30 min cook.  
https://www.sbs.com.au/food/the-cook-up-with-adam-liaw/recipe/coconut-chicken-noodle-soup/ctj3ibw91

# 9. Georgian shkmeruli — absurd garlic payoff

Shkmeruli is Georgian chicken in a garlic-heavy milk/butter sauce.

A modern reference is around **15 minutes prep / 1 hour total**, with only a handful of ingredients.

## Why this is extremely relevant

The user already likes freshly grated garlic enough to make it part of the baseline meal.

Shkmeruli turns that preference into the entire identity of the dish:

- chicken
- a lot of garlic
- milk
- butter
- salt / pepper

That is an unusually small ingredient list for something that feels like destination food.

## Friction

The classic version uses a whole butterflied chicken and a pan-sear before baking.

## Project adaptation

Test with skin-on thighs or boneless thighs before committing to whole-chicken handling.

The milk sauce needs a little care to avoid curdling, so this is **normal cooking pays / guest mode**, not control-level.

**References:**
- No Frills Kitchen — Shkmeruli, ~15 min prep / 1 hr total: https://nofrillskitchen.com/shkmeruli-recipe/
- Georgian Eats — Shkmeruli, 2026: https://georgianeats.com/recipes/shkmeruli/

# 10. Hungarian chicken paprikash — comfort-food lane without red meat

Chicken paprikash is chicken stewed with onion and lots of Hungarian paprika, often finished with sour cream and served with egg noodles / nokedli.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- soft chicken + sauce + noodles
- simple pantry profile
- strong leftovers / freezer potential
- paprika is the main identity ingredient
- a nice cold-weather alternative to curry

## Project simplification

- boneless thigh + breast blend if desired
- frozen onion
- good Hungarian sweet paprika
- sour cream or skyr/yogurt finish
- packaged egg noodles

This could become a very practical European comfort-food rotation.

**Reference:** Hungarian Eats — Light and Easy Chicken Paprikash; leftovers 3–4 days / freezer-friendly.  
https://hungarianeats.com/light-and-easy-chicken-paprikash/

# 11. Thai massaman curry — curry + potatoes + peanuts = bulk-friendly

Massaman curry combines curry paste, coconut milk, chicken, potatoes, peanuts, fish sauce, sugar and tamarind.

A shortcut Food Network version is essentially one saucepan with curry paste + coconut milk, then potatoes/peanuts, then chicken.

Thai Select Canada lists a standard version at about **15 min prep + 30 min cooking**.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- starch inside the curry
- peanuts increase calorie density
- prepared paste carries most of the flavour work
- leftovers should be very good
- gentle / rich profile offers contrast with sharper Thai red curry

## Project version

Use:

- boneless thighs
- bought massaman paste
- canned coconut milk
- baby potatoes / pre-cut potatoes
- roasted peanuts
- tamarind concentrate
- rice only if more calories are wanted

**References:**
- Food Network — Shortcut Chicken Massaman Curry: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/shortcut-chicken-massaman-curry-3364850
- Thai Select Canada — Massaman Gai: https://www.thaiselect.ca/recipes-massaman-gai

# 12. Turkish menemen — simple egg food with a real identity

Menemen is soft scrambled egg cooked with tomato and green pepper, often with butter/olive oil and chile.

Food Network versions run roughly **20–30 minutes**.

## Why it fits

- eggs as the protein
- one pan
- bread / pita carries calories
- soft texture
- brunch / low-meat dinner lane

## Project simplification

Use frozen diced peppers and canned tomatoes when fresh prep sounds annoying.

This is probably not phone-compatible in the same way as a braise because the cook time is active, but it is still low-skill and low-cleanup.

**Reference:** Food Network — Menemen, ~20 min.  
https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/menemen-12078168

# 13. Spanish tortilla — batch egg food, plus a hilarious shortcut branch

Spanish tortilla / tortilla española is egg + potato + olive oil, traditionally cooked as a thick omelet.

The classic method can be somewhat hands-on, but oven finishes remove the nerve-wracking flip.

There is also a Food Network shortcut that uses **kettle chips instead of raw potatoes**, then finishes the tortilla in the oven.

## Why it belongs in the project

- eggs as main protein
- calorie-dense from potato + oil
- good warm or room-temp
- can be cut into fridge portions
- guest/brunch friendly

## Two branches

### Proper-ish branch

Thin potatoes + egg + onion, stove then oven.

### Chaos shortcut branch

Egg + kettle chips + softened onion + paprika, oven finish.

This is exactly the kind of shortcut worth testing empirically rather than judging from principle.

**References:**
- Food Network — Easy Spanish Tortilla Española: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/easy-spanish-tortilla-espanola-3364857
- Food Network — Spanish Tortilla oven-finish method: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/spanish-tortilla-recipe2-1973781

# 14. Georgian chakhokhbili — tomato-herb chicken stew

Chakhokhbili is a Georgian chicken stew with onions, tomatoes, garlic, herbs and chile.

A 2026 Georgian Eats guide describes it as ordinary Georgian home cooking: chicken + lots of tomato/onion, simmered under an hour.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- one pot
- rich tomato sauce without dairy/coconut
- leftovers
- distinctly herbier and brighter than Italian cacciatore

## Friction warning

A generous amount of fresh herbs is part of the appeal.

Use frozen/pre-chopped herbs only if they taste acceptable; otherwise save this for a week when buying cilantro/parsley/basil sounds enjoyable.

**Reference:** Georgian Eats — Chakhokhbili, 2026.  
https://georgianeats.com/recipes/chakhokhbili/

# New meta-insight: meal families travel better than recipes

A richer mental model is emerging.

Instead of memorizing isolated dishes, recognize **families**:

### Chicken + rice in one vessel

- Hainanese chicken rice
- Puerto Rican arroz con pollo
- Brazilian galinhada
- Spanish chicken rice
- Mexican one-pot chicken rice

### Chicken + acid + onion braise

- Filipino adobo
- Senegalese yassa
- Greek lemon chicken
- Moroccan lemon/olive chicken

### Coconut + protein + starch

- Thai curry
- massaman
- laksa
- Burmese ohn no khao swè
- Sri Lankan curry
- Kerala fish curry
- Brazilian moqueca

### Soft spiced legume bowl

- Turkish red lentil soup
- Ethiopian misir wat
- dal / tofu curry lanes

### Garlic-dairy chicken

- Georgian shkmeruli
- yogurt-marinated shawarma-ish chicken
- creamy European chicken dishes

Once the family is understood, new cuisines stop feeling like unrelated recipes. They become different solutions to the same cooking problem.

That should make the compendium more useful than a giant flat list.
