# Experiment 002T: World Tour — High-Payoff Branches

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D expansion

## Goal

Keep expanding the food universe beyond the current East-Asian / curry / pasta core, while preserving the project's actual preferences:

- white-meat / fish / tofu first
- phone-compatible cooking
- low hand-fuss
- batchability when useful
- enough calorie density for bulking
- genuinely different flavour identities
- occasional guest-worthy payoff

The target is not authenticity maximalism. The target is to learn from excellent dishes around the world, then adapt them honestly to the user's workflow.

# 1. Portuguese peri-peri chicken + lemon potatoes

A very recent RecipeTin Eats one-pan Portuguese chicken and potato bake is almost suspiciously aligned with this project:

- peri-peri-style spice rub
- chicken
- sliced potatoes
- garlic
- lemon
- one pan
- oven does most of the work

The potatoes roast underneath the chicken and absorb the drippings.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- big flavour from pantry spices
- one pan
- high calorie density without needing a separate starch
- looks more guest-worthy than the effort suggests
- excellent phone compatibility once it is in the oven

## Project version

Use boneless thighs if eating speed wins over the visual effect of bone-in pieces. If slicing potatoes feels annoying, test baby potatoes or pre-cut potatoes.

**Reference:** RecipeTin Eats — One-pan Portuguese Chicken and Potato Bake, published Aug 31 2026 / updated Sep 4 2026.

# 2. Vietnamese lemongrass chicken

RecipeTin Eats' Vietnamese lemongrass chicken uses a marinade built around:

- chicken thigh
- lemongrass
- garlic
- lime
- fish sauce
- soy
- sugar

The author specifically notes that **lemongrass paste works well** when fresh lemongrass is inconvenient, and the chicken can be cooked on grill, stove, oven or broiler.

## Why it fits

This is a Ziploc recipe.

- chicken in bag
- lemongrass paste
- squeeze/pour seasonings
- squish
- marinate
- air-fry or broil

Serve with rice, noodles, premade pickles/slaw, or just arugula + lime.

This could become one of the strongest new air-fryer flavour lanes because it tastes extremely different from shawarma, miso, gochujang and adobo while using the same chicken-thigh workflow.

**Reference:** RecipeTin Eats — Vietnamese Noodles with Lemongrass Chicken, ~20 min prep / 10 min cook.

# 3. Korean dakbokkeumtang

Maangchi's traditional dakbokkeumtang is a spicy chicken-and-potato braise using:

- chicken wings/thighs
- gochujang
- gochugaru
- soy
- garlic
- sugar
- onions
- potatoes

Cooking is roughly 45–60 minutes, but most of that is covered braising.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- rich sauce + soft potatoes
- batchable
- low eating friction if using boneless thighs
- strong leftovers
- uses gochujang already in the condiment library

## Project simplification

Use boneless thighs, frozen onion, baby potatoes or pre-cut potatoes, jarred garlic, and skip decorative green-chile/scallion chopping on lazy nights.

This becomes:

> dump / stir → simmer → phone → add potatoes → simmer → eat

**Reference:** Maangchi — Traditional-style Dakbokkeumtang, updated Mar 17 2024.

# 4. One-pan baked butter chicken

RecipeTin Eats has a streamlined **one-pan baked butter chicken** with roughly:

- 10 minutes prep
- 45 minutes oven time
- everything goes into one pan

The appeal is obvious: the sauce develops while the chicken bakes, with much less stovetop attention than a classic version.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- highly palatable
- guest-friendly
- excellent with rice or naan
- 10-minute setup / long unattended window
- leftovers should be excellent

This may be a better default butter-chicken branch than the earlier skillet version.

**Reference:** RecipeTin Eats — One-pan Baked Butter Chicken.

# 5. Greek lemon chicken + potatoes

The Mediterranean Dish's Greek chicken and potatoes uses a lemon-garlic marinade and is explicitly framed as easy, comforting, and even better as leftovers the next day.

## Why it fits

- lemon + garlic + olive oil is a flavour family the project underuses
- chicken + potatoes gives a complete meal from one roasting vessel
- easy to marinate in a bag
- leftovers improve rather than merely survive

Use a bought Greek seasoning blend if measuring multiple herbs feels annoying.

**Reference:** The Mediterranean Dish — Greek Chicken and Potatoes, updated Feb 10 2026.

# 6. Greek avgolemono

Avgolemono is a silky Greek chicken-rice soup enriched with egg and lemon.

A weeknight version from The Mediterranean Dish is about **35 minutes total** and can use shredded rotisserie chicken.

## Why it is interesting

- rice + chicken + egg already fit the project
- silky soup texture is extremely low-chew
- lemon makes a rich bowl easy to keep eating
- uses leftovers / rotisserie chicken well
- freezes reasonably according to the source

## Friction warning

The egg-lemon mixture needs tempering with hot broth. That is one real finesse step.

This makes avgolemono a **normal-cooking-pays** dish rather than control-level convenience.

**Reference:** The Mediterranean Dish — Avgolemono Soup, 35 min.

# 7. Shrimp saganaki

Shrimp saganaki is shrimp simmered briefly in a tomato-garlic sauce with feta, herbs and optional olives.

The Mediterranean Dish version is:

- 10 min prep
- 20 min cook
- 30 min total

Frozen peeled/deveined shrimp work well.

## Why it fits

- seafood variety beyond salmon
- vivid tomato + feta profile
- guest-worthy skillet presentation
- excellent with orzo, couscous, bread or rice
- shrimp cooks in only a few minutes

## Project simplification

Replace chopped onion with frozen diced onion and use garlic paste. Fresh herbs become optional finishing splurge rather than requirement.

**Reference:** The Mediterranean Dish — Shrimp Saganaki, updated Nov 10 2025.

# 8. Turkish red lentil soup

Turkish mercimek çorbası is a velvety red-lentil soup that comes together in about 30 minutes.

## Why it fits

- red lentils dissolve into a smooth soup
- cheap
- fibre/protein without chewing
- easy to batch
- brightened with lemon and Aleppo pepper

## Bulk adaptation

The soup itself is not calorie-dense enough for the whole mission, so pair it with:

- buttered/olive-oil pita
- yogurt/skyr
- eggs
- chicken
- cheese

This is a useful lesson: **a meal can be nutritionally modular instead of forcing every bowl to do everything.**

**Reference:** The Mediterranean Dish — Turkish Red Lentil Soup, ~30 min.

# 9. West-African-inspired peanut stew

Budget Bytes' peanut stew is inspired by West African groundnut stews and uses:

- tomato paste
- peanut butter
- sweet potato
- greens
- spices
- broth

It is around **10 min prep / 45 min cook** and freezes very well.

## Why it fits

Peanut butter is already in the pantry.

The sauce is rich, creamy, calorie-friendly and completely different from the project's other stews.

## Project branches

- plant-based original
- chicken-thigh version
- tofu version

Use frozen spinach instead of chopping collards if convenience wins.

**Reference:** Budget Bytes — Vegan Peanut Stew, 55 min total, freezer-friendly.

# 10. Moroccan chicken with lemon, olives + dried fruit

A Moroccan chicken braise built around warm spice, lemon, olives and dried fruit has huge flavour payoff from mostly unattended cooking.

The Mediterranean Dish's version uses chicken, Ras el Hanout-style spices, lemon/preserved lemon, olives, raisins/apricots, tomato paste and broth.

## Why it fits

- chicken-first
- guest-worthy aroma and presentation
- dried fruit adds concentrated calories and sweetness
- couscous gives an almost-zero-effort starch option
- one braiser / pot

## Project simplification

Use frozen onion and a premixed Ras el Hanout blend. Keep cilantro/almonds as optional finishers.

**Reference:** The Mediterranean Dish — Moroccan Chicken.

# 11. Georgian satsivi — fascinating guest-food outlier

Satsivi is Georgian chicken or turkey in a spiced walnut sauce, traditionally served chilled or room-temperature after resting.

It commonly uses:

- poultry
- walnuts
- garlic
- onion
- coriander
- blue fenugreek / khmeli suneli
- vinegar

## Why it is interesting

- extremely distinctive
- walnuts make the sauce calorie-dense
- made ahead by design
- improves after chilling/resting
- looks and tastes like special-occasion food

## Why it is not a daily candidate

- food processor / walnut grinding
- specialty spices
- more steps than the normal project sweet spot

This is a **guest-night / curiosity branch**, not a weekday backbone.

**References:** No Frills Kitchen — Satsivi; Georgian Eats — Satsivi.

# 12. Chicken tortilla / taco soup

Budget Bytes has two useful versions:

### Slow-cooker chicken tortilla soup

- ~10 min prep
- 4–8 hr unattended slow cooking
- beans
- corn
- tomatoes/chiles
- chicken

### 2026 chicken taco soup

- 10 min prep
- 50 min cook
- large 10-serving batch
- chicken, beans, corn, peppers, cream cheese, lime/cilantro finish

## Why it fits

- batchable
- freezer-friendly
- low chopping if using frozen onion/pepper
- fibre comes naturally from beans
- toppings supply the fun: cheese, avocado/guac, lime, tortilla chips, skyr

This extends the Latin lane into a **soup/stew format** rather than another burrito bowl.

**References:** Budget Bytes — Slow Cooker Chicken Tortilla Soup; Chicken Taco Soup (2026).

# New meta-insight

We now have enough cuisines that the menu does not need to rotate by **protein** alone.

The same chicken thigh can become:

- Vietnamese lemongrass
- Portuguese peri-peri
- Korean gochujang braise
- Greek lemon-garlic
- Indian butter chicken
- Moroccan lemon/olive
- Mexican salsa/chipotle
- Middle-Eastern shawarma
- Filipino adobo

That means variety can come from the **flavour system**, while groceries remain boring and efficient.

This may be one of the project's strongest overall findings.
