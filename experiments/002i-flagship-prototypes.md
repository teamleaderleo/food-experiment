# Experiment 002I: Flagship Prototypes

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** ready for kitchen testing

## Goal

Take the three strongest meal ideas and turn them into recipes that are actually worth cooking repeatedly:

1. Taiwanese braised pork rice
2. Dan dan noodles
3. Shawarma chicken rice

These are intentionally **delicious-first** builds. Cost substitutions live in their own sections. If a cheaper substitution makes the dish worse, it loses.

The quantities below are large bulking portions. A conventional serving is roughly half. Exact calories/macros should be recalculated from the packages actually purchased.

# Prototype A — Taiwanese rou zao fan / lu rou fan lane

## Important naming note

For a weeknight-friendly ground-pork version, **rou zao fan (肉燥飯)** is the more accurate model. Classic lu rou fan commonly uses small pieces of fatty pork belly and a longer braise. Both belong in the same Taiwanese braised-pork-rice family, but they should be tested separately.

### Why rou zao fan leads the first test

- ground pork needs almost no chewing
- roughly 30 minutes of braising after browning is enough
- braised eggs belong naturally
- sauce soaks into rice
- reheats extremely well
- dried shiitake + shallot + soy + five-spice create serious depth with little active work

## Bulk dinner — rou zao fan v1

### Main

- white rice — **180 g dry**
- ground pork — **225–250 g**
- dried shiitake — **15–20 g**, soaked and finely diced
- shallot — **50–70 g**, or frozen onion if convenience wins
- ginger — **10 g**, minced
- neutral/canola oil — **10–15 g**
- Shaoxing wine — **25–30 mL**
- light soy sauce — **20–25 mL**
- dark soy sauce — **8–10 mL**
- oyster sauce — **10–15 mL**
- sugar — **8–12 g**
- star anise — **1 pod**
- five-spice — **small pinch, ~1/8 tsp**
- white pepper
- water / mushroom soaking liquid — **350–450 mL**
- eggs — **2**

### Small plant side

- bok choy / spinach / gai lan — **75–100 g cooked**
- pickled mustard greens or quick vinegar cabbage — **30–50 g**

The pickled component matters for a giant serving: it cuts the sweet, fatty braise and keeps the last third lively.

## Method

1. Start rice in the rice cooker.
2. Brown the ground pork in a wide pan. Let a little real browning develop before stirring constantly.
3. Add shallot/onion, ginger and shiitake. Cook until fragrant and softened.
4. Deglaze with Shaoxing wine.
5. Add light soy, dark soy, oyster sauce, sugar, star anise, five-spice, white pepper and mushroom liquid/water.
6. Simmer loosely covered for **20 minutes**.
7. Add peeled boiled eggs and simmer another **10 minutes**. Keep enough liquid that the final sauce remains spoonable.
8. Reduce briefly only if genuinely watery. The sauce should soak into rice, not turn into sticky mince.
9. Serve over soft rice with greens and something pickled.

## Palatability upgrades worth paying for

- real fried shallots as a finishing ingredient
- dried shiitake instead of button mushrooms
- good Taiwanese-style pickled greens
- pork shoulder hand-chopped once in a while for a meatier texture

## Convenience version

- frozen diced onions
- jarred minced ginger or frozen ginger portions
- store-ground pork
- rice cooker
- batch 3–4 portions of braise; cook rice fresh or freeze rice portions

## Weekend true lu rou fan branch

Use pork belly cut into tiny cubes, more aromatic spices, and a **90-minute+ low braise**. This is the luxury version rather than the daily default. It should be richer, stickier and more aromatic.

## Rough bulk-serving envelope

Depending heavily on pork fat level and rice quantity:

- **~1,500–1,800 kcal**
- **~60–75 g protein**

This is exactly the kind of main meal that pairs well with the standard milk/fruit shake later.

## First A/B tests

1. 225 g vs 250 g pork
2. fresh shallot vs frozen onion + fried-shallot finish
3. 8 g vs 12 g sugar
4. greens mixed into bowl vs separate acidic side
5. ground pork vs hand-chopped shoulder

# Prototype B — Dan dan noodles

## The non-negotiable flavour pieces

A convincing dan dan bowl needs several distinct layers:

- browned ground pork
- **sui mi ya cai** / preserved mustard greens
- sesame paste
- chili oil
- Sichuan pepper
- soy
- vinegar
- a little sugar

Deleting the preserved vegetable or numbing pepper turns it into a different noodle dish. Those ingredients are worth buying.

## Bulk dinner — dan dan v1

### Noodles

- dry wheat noodles — **170–180 g dry**
  - or an equivalent large portion of fresh alkaline/wheat noodles
- leafy greens — **75 g**, blanched in noodle water

### Pork topping

- ground pork — **180–200 g**
- sui mi ya cai — **25–35 g**
- Shaoxing wine — **10–15 mL**
- sweet bean sauce / tianmianjiang / hoisin — **10 g**
- dark soy — **5 mL**
- five-spice — tiny pinch

### Bowl sauce

- Chinese sesame paste or tahini — **30–35 g**
- chili oil — **15–20 g**, adjust to taste
- light soy — **15–20 mL**
- Chinkiang vinegar — **12–15 mL**
- sugar — **8–10 g**
- ground Sichuan pepper — **1/2–1 tsp**
- garlic — **1 clove**, finely grated or made into garlic water
- hot noodle water — **60–100 mL** to loosen

### Optional high-value finish

- scallion
- crushed peanuts — **10–15 g**
- 1–2 tea eggs / soft eggs if the day's choline/protein needs them

## Method

1. Brown pork until fragrant and a little crisp around the edges.
2. Add Shaoxing wine, sweet bean sauce, dark soy and five-spice; cook until the pork coating is concentrated.
3. Cook ya cai briefly with the pork or separately, depending on how distinct you want it.
4. In the eating bowl, whisk sesame paste, chili oil, soy, black vinegar, sugar, Sichuan pepper and garlic with hot noodle water until **loose and glossy**.
5. Boil noodles. Blanch greens in the same pot.
6. Transfer hot noodles directly into the sauce. Toss aggressively.
7. Add pork/ya cai and scallion. Add more noodle water until the final bowl is slippery rather than sticky.

## Throughput rule

The sauce should look **slightly too loose before the noodles go in**. Sesame paste thickens quickly. A dry dan dan bowl is a disaster for this project.

## Palatability upgrades worth paying for

- Chinese sesame paste for the deeper roasted flavour
- good chili oil / homemade chili oil made in batches
- genuine ya cai
- fresh noodles when convenient
- aromatic Sichuan peppercorns, ground shortly before use

## Convenience version

Keep a jar of premixed dan dan base in the fridge:

- sesame paste
- soy
- vinegar
- sugar
- chili oil

Then dinner is simply noodles + browned pork/ya cai + sauce. The pork topping can also be batch-cooked.

## Rough bulk-serving envelope

With 180 g noodles, 200 g pork, 35 g sesame paste and chili oil:

- **~1,500–1,800 kcal**
- **~65–80 g protein**

## First A/B tests

1. tahini vs Chinese sesame paste
2. 15 g vs 20 g chili oil
3. 25 g vs 35 g ya cai
4. fresh vs dry noodles
5. 0 vs 15 g crushed peanuts
6. 180 g vs 200 g pork

# Prototype C — Shawarma chicken rice

## Why this one deserves a flagship slot

- boneless chicken thighs stay juicy
- marinade can happen unattended
- hot roasting/air-frying creates browned edges
- rice carries sauce effortlessly
- tahini/toum add calories with very little chewing
- pickles + lemon prevent palate fatigue
- extremely easy to turn leftovers into wraps the next day

## Bulk dinner — shawarma rice v1

### Chicken

- boneless skinless chicken thighs — **300 g raw**
- lemon juice — **25–30 mL**
- olive/canola oil — **15 g**
- garlic — **2 cloves** or equivalent paste
- ground cumin — **1 tsp**
- ground coriander — **1 tsp**
- paprika — **1 tsp**
- turmeric — **1/2 tsp**
- garlic powder — **1/2 tsp**
- ground cloves — **small pinch**
- cinnamon or allspice — **small pinch**
- cayenne/chili — to taste
- iodized salt — measured as part of the day's salt budget
- sliced onion — optional, especially for oven/sheet-pan version

### Base

- white rice — **170–180 g dry**

### Sauces

- tahini — **25–30 g**
- toum / garlic sauce — **25–35 g**
- lemon juice + water to thin tahini

### Acid/crunch

- pickled turnip/cucumber/cabbage — **40–60 g**
- red pepper/tomato/cucumber — small amount, optional
- parsley/cilantro — optional

### Optional calorie slider

- pita/naan — **75–100 g**

## Method — air fryer

1. Slice thighs into bite-size pieces and toss with lemon, oil, garlic and spices.
2. Marinate at least 30 minutes when possible; several hours is better.
3. Air-fry in a hot, uncrowded basket, tossing once or twice, until browned at the edges and cooked through. Work in batches if crowding would steam the chicken.
4. While chicken cooks, make rice.
5. Thin tahini with lemon and water until pourable.
6. Build a rice bowl and use **more sauce than restraint suggests**. Add toum and pickles.

## Method — sheet pan

Roast sliced thighs and onion at high heat until browned, then broil briefly if needed for extra charred edges.

## Palatability upgrades worth paying for

- genuinely good toum
- pickled turnips
- fresh pita/naan
- boneless thighs instead of dry breast
- a shawarma spice blend you genuinely love if it saves measuring six spices

## Convenience version

- buy boneless thighs
- marinade in freezer bags in 2–3 meal portions
- freeze them raw in marinade
- thaw overnight, then air-fry
- keep jarred pickles, tahini and toum ready
- rice cooker handles the starch

A rotisserie-chicken emergency version can use warmed shredded chicken tossed with shawarma seasoning, lemon and a little oil.

## Rough bulk-serving envelope

With 300 g thigh, 180 g dry rice, tahini, toum and optional pita:

- **~1,700–2,100 kcal**
- **~75–95 g protein**

The optional pita is the easiest knob for moving the meal up or down several hundred calories.

## First A/B tests

1. 30-minute vs overnight marinade
2. 250 g vs 300 g chicken
3. tahini only vs tahini + toum
4. rice only vs rice + pita
5. air fryer vs sheet pan
6. homemade spice blend vs one good premixed shawarma seasoning

# Comparison

| Meal | Palatability ceiling | Eating speed | Batchability | Active work | Best feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rou zao fan | elite | elite | elite | low after prep | braise reheats beautifully |
| Dan dan noodles | elite | very high | sauce/topping batch well | medium first time | highest flavour intensity |
| Shawarma rice | elite | high | elite | low | easiest restaurant-style meal |

## Recommended testing order

### 1. Rou zao fan

Best first experiment because almost every component can be made ahead, and the ground-pork version is forgiving.

### 2. Shawarma rice

Tests the air-fryer / freezer-marinade convenience lane and gives huge flavour separation from Taiwanese food.

### 3. Dan dan noodles

Highest specialty-ingredient count, but potentially the most craveable. Once the sauce library exists, it becomes quick.

## What success looks like

For each prototype, record:

- exact package quantities
- active prep time
- total elapsed time
- eating time
- approximate calories/protein/fibre
- final-third enjoyment
- fullness at 0 / 60 / 120 / 180 min
- reheating quality
- desire to eat it again within 48 hours
- which ingredient felt like unnecessary work

The winner is the dish you begin making from memory because you actually want it.
