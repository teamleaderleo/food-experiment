# Experiment 002G: Meal Compositions

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D menu design

## Goal

Turn the nutrition work into meals that feel like actual cravings.

The target stays roughly:

- ~3,000–3,200 kcal/day to start
- ~130–145 g protein
- ~30–40 g fibre
- high eating throughput
- soft / saucy textures
- strong micronutrient coverage
- low active prep
- easy ±200 kcal adjustment

The recipes below are meal systems, not permanent recipes yet. They belong in `experiments/` until repeated use proves that they deserve promotion into `recipes/`.

## Shared daily modules

These can stay constant while the savoury meal changes.

### Thin banana-blueberry bulk shake

- whole milk — 500 mL
- banana — 150 g
- frozen blueberries — 75 g
- peanut butter — 30 g
- ground flax — 10 g
- optional carbohydrate slider if the day needs more calories

Keep it thin enough to drink easily. Add water or ice as needed.

### Daily micronutrient plugs

- ~2 g iodized table salt across the day's food, adjusted for total seasoning / sodium needs
- red pepper and/or another vitamin-C-rich produce portion
- small spinach / dark-green vegetable portion
- tomato concentrate or another carotenoid-rich sauce ingredient
- fish module about twice weekly for EPA/DHA

### Calorie slider

Use the easiest enjoyable option:

- buttered / margarined enriched toast with jam or honey
- extra pasta or rice
- low-fibre cereal + milk
- juice
- maltodextrin only if food volume becomes the real bottleneck

## Meal 1 — Spicy rosé pork rigatoni

This is the leading first test because it maps almost perfectly onto the 002E ingredient set while tasting like normal comfort food.

### Ingredients

- rigatoni / penne — 200 g dry
- ground pork — 150 g
- soy granules — 0–25 g dry, optional test variable
- red lentils — 0–20 g dry, optional test variable
- tomato paste — 70–80 g
- frozen spinach — 50 g
- frozen peas — 75–100 g
- red pepper — ~60 g, finely diced
- mozzarella — 60 g
- 2 eggs
- 25–35 g total added fat from sunflower / canola / olive oil / margarine as desired
- garlic
- chili flakes or Calabrian-style chili
- MSG
- black pepper
- iodized salt
- splash of milk or pasta water to loosen

### Method

1. Boil the pasta until soft but pleasant.
2. Brown the pork hard enough to create flavour, then add garlic and tomato paste and fry the paste for a minute.
3. Add soy granules and lentils if testing them, plus enough water to fully soften both.
4. Add spinach, peas, MSG, chili and salt. Simmer until the sauce is thick and completely integrated.
5. Add a splash of milk and most of the mozzarella. Melt it into a glossy pink-red sauce.
6. Toss with pasta and enough pasta water that every piece stays wet and slippery.
7. Top with the remaining mozzarella and two jammy / soft-boiled eggs, halved.
8. Fold the diced red pepper through at the end or serve it lightly dressed with vinegar on the side for crunch and acid.

### Palatability target

Rich, spicy, salty, cheesy and glossy. The soy/lentils should be invisible. If they become identifiable, reduce them.

### Variants

- vodka-ish: add chili and a little extra milk / creaminess
- pizza-pasta: oregano, pepperoni or sausage when cheap, extra mozzarella
- garlic-parmesan: reduce tomato, add more garlic, milk and parmesan-style cheese
- smoky: smoked paprika + chipotle

## Meal 2 — Japanese-style curry rice with pork and eggs

This is the soft-food / low-chew alternative to pasta.

### Ingredients

- white rice — enough dry rice to provide roughly the same carbohydrate load as the pasta meal
- ground pork or finely chopped chicken thigh — 150 g
- Japanese curry roux or homemade curry sauce
- frozen peas — 100 g
- spinach — 50 g
- red pepper — ~60 g, diced small
- tomato paste — 30–50 g as an umami / colour booster
- 2 eggs, soft-boiled
- optional soft tofu — 100–200 g if it improves texture and protein economics
- canola oil / margarine as calorie-density tools
- iodized salt as needed

### Method

Brown pork, add curry sauce and tomato paste, then cook the vegetables directly into the sauce until soft. Fold tofu in gently if using it. Serve over soft white rice with halved eggs on top.

### Palatability target

The rice should be thoroughly coated. Think curry lake, not dry rice beside a ladle of sauce.

### Why it belongs

- extremely soft
- easy to batch
- ground meat disappears into sauce
- tofu fits naturally
- frozen vegetables make sense in the cuisine
- rice provides a very easy calorie slider

## Meal 3 — Mapo tofu rice mountain

This is potentially the best Chinese lane for throughput: silky tofu, ground pork and rice require very little chewing.

### Ingredients

- white rice
- soft / medium tofu — 300–400 g
- ground pork — 100–150 g
- doubanjiang
- fermented black beans if desired
- garlic + ginger
- Sichuan pepper
- soy sauce
- MSG
- small amount of sugar
- cornstarch slurry
- spinach or finely chopped leafy greens — ~50 g
- red pepper can be eaten separately or swapped for another vitamin-C-rich side
- canola oil
- 2 eggs as soy-marinated / soft-boiled eggs, or moved to another eating pulse

### Method

Brown pork, fry doubanjiang / aromatics, add tofu and enough liquid for a loose sauce, then thicken lightly with cornstarch. Fold the greens in right at the end. Serve over rice with plenty of sauce.

### Palatability target

Silky, spicy, numbing, salty, savoury. Tofu should slide down with the rice. Keep the sauce loose enough that the bowl never turns dry.

### Nutrition logic

Tofu replaces some expensive meat while adding calcium if calcium-set. Pork keeps the dish deeply savoury. Rice carries calories. This version can skip soy granules entirely because tofu already fills the soybean role in a much nicer form.

## Meal 4 — Keema-dal rice with yogurt sauce

This is the meal where lentils genuinely belong instead of hiding apologetically.

### Ingredients

- white rice
- ground pork, beef, chicken or turkey — ~150 g
- red lentils — 40–60 g dry
- peas — 100 g
- spinach — 50 g
- tomato paste / canned tomato
- onion if desired
- garlic + ginger
- garam masala / cumin / coriander / turmeric / chili
- 2 eggs, boiled and halved or cooked directly in the curry
- yogurt / skyr thinned with lime, salt and water
- red pepper as a small fresh side or folded into the curry late
- added oil as needed for calorie density

### Method

Cook the ground meat with aromatics and spices. Add tomato and red lentils with water and simmer until the lentils dissolve into the sauce. Add peas and spinach. Serve over soft rice with eggs and a generous yogurt sauce.

### Palatability target

Rich and spoonable, with the lentils acting like sauce thickener rather than a giant separate serving of pulses.

### Why it belongs

This meal lets us raise the lentil dose without fighting the cuisine. It may be the cheapest high-fibre option that still feels luxurious.

## Meal 5 — Garlic-parmesan chicken and pea pasta

Use this when tomato fatigue sets in.

### Ingredients

- pasta — 200 g dry
- chicken thigh or ground chicken — 150–200 g
- peas — 100 g
- spinach — 50 g
- red pepper — 60 g
- garlic
- milk
- mozzarella + parmesan-style cheese
- black pepper
- MSG
- lemon juice
- 2 eggs: either soft-boiled on the side or use yolks to enrich the sauce carefully
- canola / sunflower / olive oil as the calorie-density blend

### Method

Cook the chicken, garlic and vegetables, then build a loose creamy sauce with milk, cheese, pasta water and seasoning. Add lemon at the end. Toss until glossy.

### Palatability target

Garlicky, creamy, peppery and bright. The lemon and red pepper keep the richness from becoming tiring.

## How to test these

For each meal, keep the shake broadly constant and record:

- active prep time
- active eating time
- calories and protein from exact package labels
- cooked food mass
- fullness at 0 / 60 / 120 / 180 minutes
- GI comfort
- final-third enjoyment
- how strongly you want the same meal again the next day
- whether the leftovers reheat well
- whether the meal works with frozen / pantry ingredients

## First testing order

1. **Spicy rosé pork rigatoni** — strongest fit with everything learned so far.
2. **Mapo tofu rice mountain** — strongest low-chew / Asian lane.
3. **Japanese curry rice** — easiest batch comfort-food lane.
4. **Keema-dal rice** — best honest use of lentils.
5. **Garlic-parmesan chicken pasta** — deliberate tomato-break option.

## Current thesis

The Ultimate Meal should become a **family of interchangeable comfort-food dinners**, all sharing a compact nutrition backbone and a thin shake.

That gives variety without sacrificing the economics or the R&D work. The winner is the meal that disappears into ordinary life.
