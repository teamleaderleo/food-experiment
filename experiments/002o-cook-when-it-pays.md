# Experiment 002O: Cook When It Pays

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** workflow preference / recipe-ranking rule

## Updated preference

Ultra-low-friction food remains valuable, but the project should not automatically prefer the easiest possible method.

**Normal cooking is welcome when the payoff is clearly worth it and storage/reheating are painless.**

That means a 30–60 minute recipe can beat rice-cooker + air-fryer food when it gives enough of the following:

- much better palatability
- several future meals
- excellent next-day quality
- meaningful flavour variety
- easier eating / boneless texture
- a dish that is difficult to reproduce with shortcuts

## Three cooking lanes

### Lane 1 — Control-level convenience

Examples:

- wings + rice
- frozen salmon + rice
- rice-cooker Hainanese chicken
- cold tofu + condiment
- frozen meatballs + sauce

Use when hunger is high and willingness to cook is low.

### Lane 2 — Normal cooking with payoff

Target roughly 20–45 active minutes, ideally producing 2–4 meals.

Strong candidates:

- rou zao fan
- chicken adobo
- Japanese curry
- butter-chicken-ish curry
- ragù / meat sauce
- mapo tofu sauce
- shawarma chicken
- Chinese eggplant + pork
- keema

These are especially attractive because leftovers are good or even better the next day.

### Lane 3 — Batch luxury

Higher first-night effort is acceptable when the dish stores beautifully and creates several easy future meals.

Examples:

- macarona béchamel
- cottage pie
- lasagna / baked pasta
- large braises
- tray bakes
- meatballs made from scratch

The question is not "did this take an hour?" The question is "what did the hour buy?"

## Storage quality is part of the recipe score

A good repeat recipe should ideally survive one or more of:

- refrigerator overnight with little quality loss
- 2–3 days refrigerated
- freezing without major texture damage
- reheating in microwave / oven / air fryer with very little work

Strong-storage foods:

- braised meat sauces
- curries
- tomato meat sauces
- adobo
- rou zao fan
- chili / keema
- baked pasta
- cottage pie
- cooked meatballs

Weak-storage foods:

- delicately set eggs
- already-dressed sesame/peanut noodles
- crispy fried foods stored in sauce
- fresh salad components
- herbs and crunchy garnishes

For weak-storage components, batch the base and finish fresh.

## Informal batch rule

A useful default is simply:

> Make enough for tonight + tomorrow. If scaling to a third or fourth serving costs almost no extra work, do it.

No formal container ritual is required.

A pot can remain the storage vessel when safe and practical; otherwise use one or two sensible containers rather than individually portioning everything.

## Payoff test

A normal-cooking dish earns its place when at least one of these is true:

1. **The meal is substantially more delicious than the shortcut version.**
2. **One cooking session creates 2–4 genuinely good meals.**
3. **The dish reheats so well that tomorrow's food feels like a feature rather than leftovers.**
4. **The recipe provides a flavour/texture experience that air-fryer + rice cannot.**
5. **The extra work is mostly unattended cooking rather than constant chopping/stirring.**

## Current highest-payoff normal-cooking candidates

### Rou zao fan

Very strong because the braise is the hard part and reheats extremely well. Rice can stay rice-cooker-simple.

### Chicken adobo

Low ingredient complexity, passive braise, excellent leftovers, built-in acidity.

### Ragù / meat sauce

Brown meat once, make a large pot, then pasta nights later become almost trivial. Can also become lasagna/baked pasta.

### Japanese / Indian curry

Sauce improves with a rest, scales easily, and reheats well. Rice or naan can be made fresh with little effort.

### Shawarma chicken

Marinate a larger amount once, cook several servings, then rotate rice / pita / wraps / salad with the same chicken.

### Meatballs

Scratch-made meatballs are more work than frozen ones but may earn it if they taste much better; freeze cooked meatballs or raw formed meatballs for later.

### Baked pasta / macarona béchamel

High first-night effort, excellent fridge/freezer payoff, very easy to reheat.

## New project rule

**Do not penalize cooking effort in isolation. Penalize effort that buys nothing.**

A 45-minute dinner that produces three excellent future meals may be more convenient in practice than a 12-minute dinner repeated three nights in a row.
