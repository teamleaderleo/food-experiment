# Experiment 002E: Micronutrient Compression

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** leading R&D draft

## Goal

Use the user's current calibration (~70.3 kg / ~155 lb, very tall and lean, lifting + cardio roughly every other day) to build a realistic starting bulk day around:

- **~3,000–3,200 kcal initially**
- **~130–145 g protein**
- **~30–40 g fibre**
- high palatability
- high eating throughput
- low chew burden
- strong micronutrient coverage from small, deliberate food modules
- low active prep
- easy ±200 kcal adjustment after scale-trend feedback

The central insight is that vegetables, seeds and fish do not need to occupy half the meal. A few concentrated foods can cover many of the awkward micronutrient jobs while pasta, dairy, meat and added fats carry most of the calorie load.

## Rabbit hole: Canada fortification is secretly useful

### Milk

Current Canadian regulations require cow's milk to contain **2 µg vitamin D per 100 mL** after the transition ending December 31, 2025.

That means **500 mL milk supplies 10 µg vitamin D** from fortification alone.

Source: Health Canada — vitamin D fortification amendments  
https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/nutrition-labelling/front-package/summary-amendments-gazette-nutrition-symbols-labelling.html

### Margarine

Current Canadian regulations require margarine to contain **26 µg vitamin D per 100 g**, and margarine is also a mandatory vitamin-A-fortified food category.

So **10 g margarine contributes ~2.6 µg vitamin D** while adding a small, nearly zero-chew calorie module.

Sources:  
https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/nutrition-labelling/front-package/summary-amendments-gazette-nutrition-symbols-labelling.html  
https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/nutrient-content/claims-reference-information

Current Maxi anchor: No Name margarine 3.17 kg — **$14.00 (~$0.44/100 g)**.

### Enriched flour / bread / pasta

Canadian white flour must contain added **thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, folic acid and iron**. Pasta can be sold as enriched when it contains the regulated enrichment package.

This makes cheap enriched white bread and enriched pasta more useful to the project than their macronutrients alone suggest.

Sources:  
https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/grain-and-bakery-products  
https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/common-name

Current Maxi anchor: No Name enriched white bread 675 g — **$2.98 (~$0.44/100 g)**.

### Iodized salt

Canadian salt sold for general household/table use is required to contain **0.01% potassium iodide**. Use measured iodized table salt as the iodine module and season the meal properly.

Source:  
https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/salt

Exact iodine intake should be calculated from the purchased salt label / regulatory composition while also tracking total sodium.

## The Four-Colour Micro-Stack

The goal is broad plant coverage with very little food mass.

### 1. Red sweet pepper — ~60 g

Health Canada's common-food table lists half a raw red pepper (60 g) at:

- ~15 kcal
- ~113 mg vitamin C
- ~93 µg vitamin A RAE
- ~126 mg potassium

For reference, Health Canada's adult male vitamin-C RDA is 90 mg/day. One small half pepper can therefore carry the vitamin-C job almost by itself.

Source:  
https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/healthy-eating/nutrient-data/table-4-vegetables-vegetable-products-nutrient-value-some-common-foods-2008.html

### 2. Frozen / cooked spinach — ~50 g

Health Canada's boiled-spinach entry is extremely dense: 95 g supplies roughly:

- 129 mg calcium
- 3.4 mg iron
- 443 mg potassium
- 83 mg magnesium
- 498 µg vitamin A RAE
- 139 µg folate DFE
- 9 mg vitamin C

Using ~50 g gives substantial A/K/folate/magnesium coverage while disappearing into pasta sauce.

Current Walmart anchor: Great Value frozen chopped spinach 500 g — **$3.37 (~$0.67/100 g)**.

### 3. Tomato concentrate — ~80 g

Processed tomato products are among the concentrated dietary sources of lycopene. Use tomato paste / concentrated tomato sauce for flavour, potassium and carotenoid diversity with much less volume than a pile of fresh tomatoes.

Health Canada notes that processed tomato products such as juice, sauce, paste and ketchup contain high amounts of lycopene.

### 4. Blueberries — ~75 g

Use a modest frozen-berry portion for anthocyanin/polyphenol variety. This is a targeted plant-compound module, not an attempt to chase a fake universal "antioxidant score."

The quantity is deliberately smaller than earlier drafts to control cost, fibre and shake thickness.

## Fat as a micronutrient-delivery system

Instead of treating every gram of added fat as interchangeable, give each fat a job.

### Sunflower oil — ~15 mL

Health Canada's common-food table gives 15 mL sunflower oil approximately:

- 122 kcal
- **5.7 mg vitamin E**

Vitamin E is otherwise somewhat annoying to cover compactly. Sunflower oil can carry a meaningful fraction with zero chewing.

### Canola oil — ~15 mL

Health Canada gives 15 mL canola oil approximately:

- 125 kcal
- **2.4 mg vitamin E**
- a mixed mono/polyunsaturated fat profile

### Margarine — ~10 g

Use mainly for regulated vitamin A + D fortification and taste.

### Extra-virgin olive oil — ~5 g

Keep a small amount for flavour / olive polyphenols if it improves the sauce. It no longer needs to be the main calorie oil.

This is an R&D fat blend, not a sacred formula. A/B taste, appetite recovery and cost.

## Fish becomes a rotation module

Daily sardines were doing too many jobs in earlier drafts.

Current Canadian fortification makes vitamin D easier to cover through milk + margarine, so fish can focus on **EPA/DHA, B12, selenium and culinary variety**.

Health Canada's sardine table shows why they remain excellent:

- Pacific sardines in tomato, 106 g can: ~197 kcal, 22 g protein, 254 mg calcium, 12.7 µg vitamin D, 9.54 µg B12
- Atlantic sardines in oil, 106 g can: ~220 kcal, 26 g protein, 405 mg calcium, 2.5 µg vitamin D, 9.48 µg B12

Source:  
https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/healthy-eating/nutrient-data/table-8-fish-shellfish-nutrient-value-some-common-foods-2008.html

Test fish **2–3 times/week** first instead of forcing it into every daily prototype. Exact EPA/DHA strategy can be refined later.

## Leading Prototype 002E

This is the first formula that looks like an actual candidate instead of a stunt.

### Savoury — high-throughput enriched pasta

- enriched dry pasta — **200 g**
- ground pork — **150 g raw**
- textured soy granules — **25 g dry**
- red lentils — **20 g dry**
- tomato paste / concentrate — **80 g**
- frozen spinach — **50 g**
- frozen peas — **100 g**
- raw red sweet pepper — **60 g**, finely chopped or served as the crisp/acidic element
- mozzarella — **60 g**
- sunflower oil — **15 g-ish / ~15 mL test portion**
- canola oil — **15 g-ish / ~15 mL test portion**
- margarine — **10 g**
- extra-virgin olive oil — **5 g**
- eggs — **2**
- iodized salt
- MSG
- garlic, chili, herbs, acidity to taste

Design principle: everything except the pepper should be **soft, finely textured and aggressively sauced**. Soy and lentils should disappear into the meat sauce.

### Sweet/liquid — thin bulk shake

- whole milk — **500 mL**
- banana — **150 g**
- frozen blueberries — **75 g**
- peanut butter — **30 g**
- ground flax — **10 g**
- carbohydrate slider — **up to ~400 kcal** from the easiest tolerated source

#### Carbohydrate-slider candidates

A/B test these rather than canonizing one immediately:

1. **maltodextrin** — maximum drinking throughput / minimum chew; emergency afterburner
2. **enriched white bread + margarine/jam** — cheap, fortified, highly palatable, still easy to eat
3. **extra pasta** — keeps the food conventional but adds cooked mass
4. **low-fibre cereal + milk** — easy eating and often fortified
5. **juice / other drinkable carbohydrate** — high throughput but can become expensive

The default experiment should begin with real-food starch/bread. Maltodextrin earns a permanent role only if solid-food volume becomes the actual bottleneck.

## Rough macro envelope

Using representative values for this exact draft **with a 400-kcal carbohydrate slider**:

- **~3,215 kcal**
- **~135 g protein**
- **~35 g fibre**

These are planning figures. Calculate the actual prototype from the exact labels and weighed portions before recording results.

## Rough current cost floor

Using the current sale / wholesale anchors already collected in this repo, the consumed-food math for this draft comes out around **~$7/day** at the aggressive theoretical end.

That number assumes several strong sale/wholesale prices at once. A sane retail expectation is more like **~$8–10/day**, depending on meat, dairy, berries and oil prices.

Price remains secondary to palatability and throughput once we are in this range.

## Why the formula got easier after personal calibration

At ~70.3 kg, the R&D protein target is now around **130–145 g/day** instead of trying to force 180–220 g.

That lets us:

- reduce soy
- reduce lentils
- remove heroic milk-powder doses
- keep the shake thinner
- spend more calories on starch / enjoyable fats
- reduce fibre pressure
- keep protein comfortably high for a lifting-oriented bulk

## Eating-rate hypothesis

Recent controlled feeding work shows that food texture and eating rate can materially change spontaneous energy intake even when diets are otherwise matched. Softer / faster-eating foods make it easier to get more energy down.

Source example: 2026 AJCN randomized crossover trial  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41314613/

For this project, use that insight deliberately:

- soft pasta
- finely minced sauce
- minimal dry chewing
- thin shake
- high energy density
- enough flavour contrast that the last third remains appealing

## Hard R&D metrics for Prototype 002E

Record:

- exact package labels and weights
- total cooked / ready-to-eat mass
- calories / protein / fibre
- micronutrient estimate from actual brands
- total cost consumed
- savoury eating time
- shake drinking time
- kcal per active consumption minute
- appetite/fullness at 0 / 60 / 120 / 180 minutes
- GI comfort
- thirst
- desire to eat the same thing again tomorrow
- weight trend over subsequent weeks once the daily calorie target is repeated consistently

## Immediate A/B tests

1. **Carb slider:** bread vs maltodextrin vs extra pasta.
2. **Flax:** 10 g vs 0 g in shake — does viscosity change meaningfully?
3. **Soy:** 25 g vs 0 g — does it earn its tiny cost/texture footprint?
4. **Lentils:** 20 g vs 0 g — can we keep folate/iron/fibre while remaining invisible?
5. **Fat distribution:** 30 g added fat in pasta vs split between pasta + bread/shake.
6. **Sunflower oil:** keep for vitamin E vs use sunflower kernels for crunch.
7. **Margarine:** confirm purchased label, then test 10–15 g as a D/A-fortification/calorie plug.
8. **Fish cadence:** 0 vs 2–3 sardine/mackerel meals per week.
9. **Pepper format:** raw diced vs blended into sauce — vitamin C coverage versus chewing/taste.
10. **One eating pulse vs two:** pasta + shake together versus separated by ~1–2 hours.

## Current thesis

The likely winner is becoming pleasantly ordinary:

> **A giant, extremely saucy meat/soy/lentil pasta dinner + a thin banana-blueberry milkshake, with tiny micronutrient modules hidden inside.**

The scientific-abomination part lives in the arithmetic. The food itself should taste like pasta and a milkshake.
