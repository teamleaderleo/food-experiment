# Experiment 002D: Personal Targets for the Ultimate Meal

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D calibration

## Current self-reported baseline

- Height: about **6'3"–6'4"**
- Body weight: about **155 lb when fully fed/hydrated**
- Converted body weight: about **70.3 kg**
- Training: roughly **every other day**, combining weights and cardio
- Current context: recently lighter after a vacation with less training; now returning to normal training
- Goal: **bulk / gain body mass while preserving training performance and keeping food easy to eat**

## What this changes

The earlier 180–220 g protein design target was too high for a 70.3 kg body weight.

Using the usual evidence-based resistance-training range as a design anchor:

- 1.6 g/kg = about **112 g/day**
- 1.8 g/kg = about **127 g/day**
- 2.0 g/kg = about **141 g/day**
- 2.2 g/kg = about **155 g/day**

### New protein target

Design the default Ultimate Meal around **130–145 g protein/day**.

Treat **~155 g/day** as a practical upper design boundary for normal prototypes. Going higher can happen incidentally, but there is no reason to force 180–220 g into the recipe simply to hit an old spreadsheet target.

This is a major throughput win because excess protein is relatively expensive, filling, and often attached to food volume.

## Calorie target: use the scale as the feedback loop

Height/activity information alone is not enough to derive a trustworthy maintenance number without age and more detailed activity data.

For R&D, use an intake target that is easy to adjust from observed body-weight trend.

### Starting engineering target

Aim the first complete prototypes around **~3,000–3,200 kcal/day**.

Then track morning body weight under reasonably consistent conditions and use a 7-day average.

Desired initial trend for a controlled bulk:

- roughly **+0.25 to +0.75 lb/week**

Adjustment rule:

- if the 7-day average is essentially flat for ~2 weeks, add about **150–250 kcal/day**
- if weight is climbing much faster than desired for multiple weeks, remove about **150–250 kcal/day**

The recipe therefore needs modular calorie dials rather than one sacred calorie number.

## Preferred calorie dials

Low-chew additions/removals that change energy without rebuilding the meal:

- olive oil: ±10–15 g
- peanut butter: ±20–30 g
- milk / dairy powder concentration
- pasta/rice: ±25–50 g dry
- cheese: ±20–30 g
- banana / other easy carbohydrate
- optional maltodextrin only if whole-food throughput has already hit its practical limit

## Updated daily targets for Prototype 002B/C successors

- **Energy:** start around 3,000–3,200 kcal
- **Protein:** **130–145 g**
- **Fibre:** roughly **30–40 g**
- high carbohydrate availability for lifting/cardio
- enough fat to keep food volume manageable
- strong calcium, iron, zinc, magnesium, potassium, folate, iodine, selenium, choline, vitamins A/C/D/E/K/B12, and omega-3 coverage
- total active eating/drinking target: **<30–35 minutes**
- appetite should recover comfortably enough that another eating opportunity does not feel impossible

## Likely recipe consequence

Cut protein excess first.

For the throughput pasta prototype, that means likely reductions such as:

- less soy granule quantity
- less milk powder / concentrated dairy
- smaller meat serving when cheese/dairy/fish are already contributing

Spend the freed calories on easier carbohydrate and moderate fat.

The likely personal optimum should feel more like:

> carbohydrate-heavy, protein-sufficient, calorie-dense, highly palatable

than:

> bodybuilder protein challenge.

## Measurement plan

For each prototype, record:

- morning body weight
- 7-day average body weight
- calories actually eaten
- protein and fibre
- workout day / rest day
- hunger before eating
- active eating/drinking time
- fullness at 0 / 60 / 120 / 180 minutes
- gastrointestinal comfort
- appetite later that day
- training performance / energy subjectively

## Current thesis

At ~70 kg body weight, the hardest problem is **getting enough enjoyable energy consistently**, not forcing heroic protein quantities.

That makes the Ultimate Meal easier to design: lower protein, moderate fibre, more carbohydrate, high palatability, and calorie modules that can be adjusted by ~200 kcal at a time from real weight-trend data.
