# Experiment 002: The Ultimate Meal

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D

## Question

Can one deliberately engineered meal cover essentially an entire bulking day at once: lots of calories, lots of protein, serious fibre, broad micronutrient coverage, several useful plant compounds, and enough actual deliciousness that eating it feels appealing?

The goal is an **abomination with intent**: one food object or one giant bowl, assembled from ordinary groceries, that is absurdly nutritionally dense and convenient.

## Initial design targets

These are R&D targets, not fixed personal requirements. Tune them once an actual daily calorie/protein target is established.

- **Energy:** roughly 3,000–3,500 kcal
- **Protein:** roughly 170–220+ g
- **Fibre:** roughly 40–60 g
- **High carbohydrate availability** for bulking and training fuel
- **Enough fat** to make high calories achievable without impossible food volume
- Broad coverage of:
  - calcium
  - iron
  - zinc
  - magnesium
  - potassium
  - folate
  - iodine
  - selenium
  - choline
  - vitamins A, C, D, E, K and B12
  - omega-3 fats
- Plant diversity from several families rather than chasing one meaningless "antioxidant score"
- Active preparation should remain low
- Ingredients should overlap normal groceries
- It has to taste good enough to crave again

## Candidate v0: Nutritional Singularity Bowl

### Calorie + carbohydrate chassis

- cooked rice, ~2–2.5 cups
- oats, ~60 g, toasted or otherwise incorporated
- avocado, 1
- extra-virgin olive oil, ~2 tbsp

### Protein block

- cooked chicken thighs, ~200 g
- black beans, ~1 cup cooked
- eggs, 2
- plain skyr, ~400 g
- canned salmon or sardines, 1 can

### Plant / fibre block

- spinach
- red bell pepper
- red cabbage
- tomato
- frozen berries, ~150–200 g

### Seeds / dense extras

- ground flax, ~15 g
- hemp hearts, ~20 g
- sunflower seeds, ~20 g
- cacao nibs, ~15 g

### Flavour system

- iodized salt
- lime
- garlic
- cumin
- chili / hot sauce
- optional cilantro

## The central culinary problem

Putting all of these ingredients into the same bowl can easily produce cafeteria sludge. The experiment only succeeds if the flavour system gives the ingredients a reason to be together.

### Direction A — Mole-ish burrito singularity

- rice + black beans as base
- chicken + egg + fish as protein components
- spinach / cabbage / pepper / tomato cooked or chopped into the savory part
- cacao + chili + cumin + garlic used toward a mole-ish flavour profile
- skyr + lime + salt as crema
- berries converted into a spicy berry-lime salsa so they belong in the same meal
- seeds used as crunchy topping
- avocado folded in at the end

This is the leading concept because nearly every odd ingredient can be made legible inside a Mexican/Latin-ish flavour family.

### Direction B — Giant burrito / tortilla brick

Same ingredient logic, but use several tortillas and turn the filling into one huge burrito assembly. Easier to portion while still counting as one engineered meal.

Potential problem: enough tortillas to contain the whole thing may add an enormous amount of extra food volume.

### Direction C — Maximum grocery efficiency version

Simplify to the ingredients doing the most work per dollar and per unit of attention:

- rice
- dried beans
- oats
- milk
- whey
- eggs
- frozen spinach
- frozen berries
- sunflower seeds
- flax
- olive oil
- iodized salt
- canned fatty fish

This branch is less committed to being one coherent cuisine. Its question is how cheaply the nutritional targets can be reached with normal groceries.

### Direction D — Cursed convenience branch

Use the **$13.99 Domino's loaded large** as the calorie/protein chassis, then engineer one dense companion component using skyr, oats, flax, berries, spinach and other high-value ingredients to patch fibre and micronutrient gaps.

This is philosophically impure because it becomes two components. Keep it because it may win on real-life convenience and dollars.

## R&D variables

Change one or two at a time.

### Protein

- chicken thigh vs breast
- sardines vs salmon
- eggs vs more skyr
- black beans vs pinto beans
- whey as an optional efficiency tool

### Calorie density

- olive oil quantity
- avocado
- seed quantity
- rice quantity
- tortilla quantity

### Fibre / plant volume

- black beans
- oats
- berries
- cabbage
- spinach
- flax

Fibre is useful; excessive fibre can make a giant bulking meal physically difficult. Find the point where the bowl remains comfortable to eat.

### Flavour

- garlic-parmesan-adjacent creamy direction
- mole-ish cacao/chili direction
- lime-skyr crema
- berry salsa
- pickled onion / cabbage
- jalapeño / chipotle

### Texture

A successful version should contain at least three textures:

- soft/starchy base
- juicy/creamy component
- crisp/crunchy component

## First prototype

Do **not** buy the entire ingredient list specifically for Prototype 1.

Use what is already at home and fill obvious gaps during a normal grocery run.

Suggested first pass:

1. rice + black beans
2. chicken
3. spinach or another available vegetable
4. skyr-lime sauce
5. berries made into salsa or eaten inside the same bowl experiment
6. flax + hemp + sunflower seeds
7. olive oil
8. iodized salt + chili + cumin + garlic

Add fish, eggs, avocado, cabbage, pepper, cacao and the rest in later iterations if they improve coverage without wrecking taste or volume.

## Measurement plan

For every prototype, record:

- exact ingredient weights / package serving sizes
- calories
- protein
- fibre
- approximate cost
- active cooking time
- total elapsed time
- how much of the bowl was comfortably edible in one sitting
- whether eating it across 2–4 hours works better
- digestion / fullness
- taste after the first third, second third and final third
- leftovers, if any

## Success criteria

The Ultimate Meal earns a permanent recipe when it:

1. reaches the intended calorie and protein range,
2. provides a genuinely high-fibre, plant-diverse day,
3. covers the obvious micronutrient weak spots using ordinary foods,
4. can be made with low active attention,
5. costs enough less than takeout to feel compelling,
6. remains enjoyable through the final bites,
7. can be repeated without requiring a ceremonial grocery expedition.

## Current thesis

The likely winner is **a giant rice-and-bean bowl with animal protein, skyr crema, fatty fish or eggs, frozen/fresh colourful plants, seeds and olive oil**, with a mole-ish / chili-lime flavour system making the strange ingredients belong together.

The limiting factor will probably be **food volume and flavour fatigue**, not the ability to cram enough nutrients into one batch.
