# Experiment 002P: Phone-Compatible Cooking

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** workflow criterion

## Core observation

Cooking time itself is not the main source of friction.

The annoying part is **manual/fussy engagement**:

- lots of chopping
- sticky mixing bowls
- breading
- shaping meatballs/patties
- repeated transferring between bowls/pans
- lots of raw-meat handling
- pan babysitting
- delicate timing
- techniques where hands stay dirty or occupied

A recipe can take 30–60 minutes and still fit daily life very well if most of that time is semi-attended or unattended.

## New criterion: phone compatibility

A meal scores well if it allows long stretches where attention can go elsewhere.

### Very good

- rice cooker
- air fryer
- oven roasting
- simmering/braising
- pressure cooker
- sheet-pan cooking
- marinading in a Ziploc bag
- baking a casserole
- reheating leftovers
- occasional stir / flip / temperature check

### Acceptable

- one skillet with a few discrete steps
- browning meat, then letting it simmer
- quick sauce mixing directly in the cooking vessel
- chopping one or two ingredients

### Annoying unless payoff is high

- constant wok/stir-fry attention
- breading stations
- hand-mixing raw meat
- shaping dumplings/meatballs
- multi-bowl sauces
- multiple cutting-board cycles
- elaborate garnishing
- recipes with several short timers running at once

## Ziploc rule

If a marinade can be made by:

1. put protein in bag
2. squirt/pour seasonings into bag
3. seal
4. squish from outside
5. refrigerate/freeze

that is strongly preferred over dirtying a bowl and manually mixing the meat.

This applies to:

- shawarma chicken
- gochujang chicken
- soy-five-spice chicken
- adobo-style chicken
- curry chicken
- honey-garlic chicken
- lemon-herb chicken
- wings / boneless thighs

Freezer version: assemble several bags at once only when it feels easy, not as a mandatory meal-prep ritual.

## Better friction metric

Instead of judging recipes mainly by elapsed cook time, think in three dimensions:

### 1. Hands-on minutes

How long are hands actually occupied?

### 2. Attention density

How often does the food demand a decision/check/stir/flip?

### 3. Mess friction

How many annoying cleanup items are created?

A 45-minute braise can beat a 20-minute stir-fry if the braise requires 8 hands-on minutes and one pot.

## The current wings still cost effort

Even the default marinated wings are not literally zero-effort:

- bag/marinade prep
- raw chicken handling
- loading air fryer
- flipping
- cleanup

They remain strong because the effort is low-skill, low-finesse and mostly compatible with doing something else.

That distinction is important.

## Recipes that now look better

This criterion increases the ranking of:

- adobo
- rou zao fan
- ragù
- Japanese curry
- butter-chicken-ish curry
- shawarma thighs from marinade bag
- sheet-pan chicken
- rice-cooker Hainanese chicken
- baked pasta
- cottage pie / macarona béchamel
- chili
- pressure-cooker braises

It lowers the ranking of recipes that need prolonged precise stovetop work unless the result is exceptional.

## Design target

The ideal recurring meal often has this shape:

> **5–10 minutes of setup → 20–45 minutes where the food mostly handles itself → 1–3 short interventions → eat now and/or store leftovers.**

If the cooking period can coexist with phone time, conversation, reading, or other low-attention tasks, elapsed time becomes much less important.

## Current thesis

The project should optimize for **low-friction attention**, not absolute minimum cook time.

A recipe earns a place when its hands-on work is simple and the payoff is high: better taste, multiple meals, better nutrition, or enough variety that the effort feels worthwhile.
