# Experiment 002W: World Tour Round 4 — Curry Powders, Egg Meals, Fish Braises + Rice Centerpieces

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D expansion

## Goal

Keep expanding the personal food atlas while getting stricter about **what kind of effort buys what kind of payoff**.

This round deliberately looked for:

- dishes that feel like a genuinely new destination
- low-fuss chicken / fish / egg / lentil meals
- one-pot or long-unattended cooking
- specialty ingredients that can unlock several dishes instead of one
- guest-worthy food where the difficulty is lower than the visual payoff suggests

Standing rules still apply:

- chicken / fish / tofu / eggs / dairy first
- pork/beef as flavour-heavy rotation foods
- phone-compatible cooking is more important than elapsed clock time
- buy away chopping when practical
- preserve the character of a dish instead of forcing every recipe into a fake 10-minute version

# 1. Sri Lankan chicken curry — roasted curry powder is the shortcut

A 2025 SBS recipe is almost comically aligned with the project on paper:

- **5 minutes prep**
- **40 minutes cooking**
- chicken thighs
- roasted Sri Lankan curry powder
- turmeric / chilli / black pepper
- lemongrass
- curry leaves
- pandan
- ginger / garlic
- vinegar

The chicken marinates, then the aromatic base cooks briefly before the whole thing is covered and left to cook until tender.

## Why it fits

The useful lesson is not “own fifteen Sri Lankan spices.”

It is:

> **buy roasted Sri Lankan curry powder and let one good blend carry most of the identity.**

Project simplification:

- boneless thighs
- roasted Sri Lankan curry powder
- lemongrass paste
- ginger / garlic paste
- frozen onion
- curry leaves from freezer
- pandan kept frozen if this lane sticks

This could be a very strong **normal-cooking-pays** curry that tastes much more specific than generic curry powder.

**Source:** SBS Food — Sri Lankan chicken curry  
https://www.sbs.com.au/food/the-cook-up-with-adam-liaw/recipe/sri-lankan-chicken-curry/r2fe4moei

# 2. Sri Lankan coconut dal — another serious red-lentil candidate

SBS has a coconut dal at:

- **10 min prep**
- **30 min cook**

The base is red lentils, coconut milk, curry leaves, garlic, chilli, lemongrass, cinnamon, turmeric and greens, finished with lime.

## Why it fits

This is exactly the legume texture we want:

- soft
- creamy
- little chewing
- reheats well
- easy to make calorie-dense with coconut milk / rice / bread

The original adds a fresh tomato sambol. That is optional in the project version. If chopping sounds annoying, use:

- lime
- pickle / chutney
- chili crisp
- purchased relish
- or simply skip the fresh side

Frozen spinach can replace hand-cut greens.

**Source:** SBS Food — Coconut dal with gai lan and tomato sambol  
https://www.sbs.com.au/food/the-cook-up-with-adam-liaw/recipe/coconut-dal-with-gai-lan-and-tomato-sambol/bi4jk6uu8

# 3. Sri Lankan egg curry — 12 eggs can actually be dinner

A simple SBS egg curry is:

- **10 min prep**
- **25 min cook**
- 12 hard-boiled eggs
- coconut milk
- turmeric
- curry leaves
- tomato / onion / chilli
- optional Maldive fish

## Why it is interesting

This is a strong answer to “I want something substantial and hot without another meat-heavy meal.”

It gives:

- eggs as main protein
- coconut curry sauce
- rice as calorie carrier
- excellent soft texture
- strong batch potential

The more elaborate Sri Lankan version deep-fries the peeled eggs. That is fun, but the simpler version proves frying is not required for the meal format.

Project version:

> boiled eggs + bought/frozen aromatic shortcuts + coconut curry + rice

This deserves a real test.

**Source:** SBS Food — Egg curry  
https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/egg-curry/zxlpv2d8k

# 6. Burmese chicken curry — learn the “oil returns” technique

A 2026 Burmese Cookbook recipe for **kyet thar hin** is built around the Burmese *si pyan* idea: onions, garlic and ginger cook with chicken until water evaporates and clear oil rises back through the finished curry.

Reference timing:

- **20 min prep**
- **70 min cook**

## Why this is worth learning

This is a technique, not merely another spice list.

The meal teaches:

> **cook the aromatic paste long enough that the sauce changes state rather than just warming ingredients together.**

That is more hands-on at the beginning, then mostly simmering.

It belongs in **normal cooking pays**, with the first run treated as a technique experiment.

Project anti-fuss move: use a food processor or prepared onion/garlic/ginger rather than hand-mincing everything.

**Source:** Burmese Cookbook — Chicken Curry, the Burmese Way  
https://burmesecookbook.com/recipes/kyet-thar-hin/

# 7. Khao soi — another coconut noodle universe

Northern Thai **khao soi** is coconut curry broth + egg noodles + chicken + acidic/pickled/crunchy toppings.

An SBS version is:

- **20 min prep**
- **20 min cook**

The important shortcut note: **ready-made red curry paste can replace the homemade khao-soi paste.**

## Project version

Skip the deep-fried noodle garnish on ordinary nights.

Use:

- red curry paste
- coconut milk
- chicken thighs
- egg noodles
- fish sauce / soy / sugar
- bought pickled cabbage
- chili oil
- lime
- fried shallots from a jar

That gets most of the restaurant-bowl experience with almost no garnish manufacturing.

This becomes a cousin of laksa and Burmese ohn-no-khao-swè, yet still tastes like its own trip.

**Source:** SBS Food — Khao soi  
https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/khao-soi-chiang-mai-coconut-curry-noodle-soup/g1lrpcu1m

# 8. Korean gyeranbap — the emergency meal is culturally real

Maangchi's gyeranbap is egg + rice + seaweed + soy + sesame oil.

Her upgraded version crisps a formed rice cake and fries the egg, but the older everyday logic she describes is even more relevant to this project:

> hot rice + egg + soy

## Project version

Do not turn this into a rice-sculpting hobby.

Use:

- hot rice
- fried egg(s)
- gim / furikake
- soy
- sesame oil
- kimchi if available

This is **control-level emergency food**.

Add cold tofu, skyr, leftover chicken or extra eggs if it needs more protein/calories.

**Source:** Maangchi — Gyeranbap  
https://www.maangchi.com/recipe/gyeranbap

# 10. Turkish çılbır — ridiculous visual payoff from yogurt + eggs

Çılbır is:

- garlicky Greek yogurt
- poached eggs
- Aleppo-pepper butter
- bread

Eggs.ca lists roughly:

- **10 minutes prep**
- **3 minutes cook**

## Why it is interesting

It looks like brunch-restaurant food while using ingredients we already like:

- yogurt
- eggs

# 11. Persian tahchin — “holy shit” baked rice

Tahchin is a saffron-yogurt rice cake layered with chicken and barberries, baked until it develops a crisp golden exterior.

The SBS version is labelled **Easy** despite:

- **30 min prep**
- **2 hr 15 min cook**

Most of that time is cooking / baking rather than delicate hand work.

## Why it is exactly the right kind of guest project

- dramatic unmolded centerpiece
- rice is the star
- chicken-first
- yogurt + saffron + barberries feel luxurious
- leftovers should be useful
- visually much more advanced than the skill level required

There is genuine rice technique here, so this should not be rushed into a weeknight hack.

This is a **weekend / guest-night skill project**.

https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/tahchin/onbkyo0bg

# 13. Rice-cooker Lebanese chicken + spiced rice

Adam Liaw has an SBS shortcut for Lebanese chicken and rice using a rice cooker:

- **10 min prep**
- **40 min cook**

The published recipe uses both chicken thighs and beef/lamb mince with baharat/allspice/nutmeg, then finishes with toasted nuts and yogurt.

## Project adaptation

Because the default is lower red-meat share:

- use ground chicken instead of beef/lamb, **or**
- omit the mince and increase chicken

The high-level idea is excellent:

> briefly brown the flavor base → transfer rice + spices to cooker → chicken on top → press button → yogurt/nuts at the end

This extends the rice cooker from “starch appliance” into “whole-dinner appliance.”

**Source:** SBS Food — Easy Lebanese chicken and rice  
https://www.sbs.com.au/food/the-cook-up-with-adam-liaw/recipe/easy-lebanese-chicken-rice/ud97r3dmz

# 14. Koshari — bulking carb chaos that might actually be useful

Egyptian koshari combines:

- rice
- lentils
- macaroni
- tomato sauce
- onions
- chile / garlic

SBS lists:

- **10 min prep**
- **45 min cook**

The traditional format has multiple components, which can create dish/pan friction.

## Why it still deserves a branch

This is almost comically compatible with the calorie mission:

- cheap starch
- lentils
- tomato sauce
- crispy onion
- huge batch potential

Project shortcut ideas:

- canned/pre-cooked lentils
- store-bought crispy onions
- jarred tomato-chile sauce
- deliberately simplify the number of pots

Do not optimize before trying a good version once. The whole point is to learn whether the weird rice+lentic+macaroni combination is craveable enough to justify the workflow.

**Source:** SBS Food — Koshari  
https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/lentils-and-rice-with-tomato-koshari/l8dqrhczx

# New meta-insight 1: spice blends are appliances

A good prepared blend can remove as much friction as a kitchen gadget.

Examples:

- roasted Sri Lankan curry powder
- Malaysian meat curry powder
- Jamaican curry powder
- Japanese curry roux
- berbere
- Ras el Hanout
- baharat

They compress:

- sourcing
- measuring
- roasting
- grinding
- decision-making

into one scoop.

The project should actively prefer a **good cuisine-specific blend** over recreating ten-spice mixes every Tuesday.
