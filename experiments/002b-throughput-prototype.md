# Experiment 002B: Throughput-Optimized Ultimate Meal

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Status:** R&D draft

## Goal

Push the Ultimate Meal toward the actual optimum for bulking:

- high palatability
- high calorie/protein delivery per minute
- moderate food mass
- enough fibre and micronutrients without turning the meal into a fermentation project
- comfortable appetite recovery
- low active prep
- current-food-cost target under about CAD $10/day where sale/wholesale pricing cooperates

This draft deliberately spends more than the mathematical lentil/soy price floor in exchange for easier eating.

## New throughput discoveries

### 1. Double-strength dairy is a serious weapon

Walmart currently lists Great Value instant skim milk powder at **$11.58 / 1 kg**. A representative label gives **90 kcal and 9 g protein per 25 g**, while the Canadian Dairy Commission lists typical skim milk powder at **34–37% protein by weight**.

Adding dry milk powder directly to ordinary milk raises calories, protein, calcium and milk solids with almost no extra liquid volume.

This is potentially more useful to this project than whey because it simultaneously does protein + calories + calcium + vitamin-D-fortified dairy work at grocery-store pricing.

### 2. Whole milk powder is a calorie-density variant

Trensité whole milk powder is currently about **$8.98 / 500 g** at Walmart and is roughly **500 kcal / 100 g**.

Skim powder is the better protein-per-dollar tool; whole powder is the richer calorie-density / flavour tool. A mixture may taste better than heroic amounts of peanut butter or oil.

### 3. Bananas are absurdly cheap liquid-friendly produce

Costco Same-Day currently lists bananas at **$2.26 / 1.36 kg (~$0.17 / 100 g)**.

They add potassium, carbohydrate, texture and sweetness to a shake with essentially zero chewing once blended.

### 4. Tomato paste beats watery tomato volume

Wholesale Club currently lists Heinz tomato paste at **$13.49 / 2.84 L (~$0.48 / 100 mL)**.

Tomato paste gives concentrated tomato flavour and micronutrient value in much less physical volume than several fresh tomatoes or a watery sauce. It can be diluted only as much as the pasta needs.

### 5. Bulk mozzarella is cheap enough to be functional

A1 currently lists Castle mozzarella at **$26.49 / 2.27 kg (~$1.17 / 100 g)** online.

At 40–60 g/day, cheese becomes a plausible tool for:

- palatability
- protein
- calcium
- calorie density
- making the soy/lentil component taste like dinner

### 6. Ground pork may beat chicken on eating pleasure per dollar

Maxi currently shows lean ground pork around **$0.89–1.00 / 100 g** depending sale/store, with medium ground pork around the same general discount-banner territory.

Ground meat mixes directly into sauce, reduces chewing compared with chunks of chicken, and contributes fat that makes a giant pasta meal easier to enjoy.

### 7. Costco rotisserie chicken is the zero-prep control

Costco Same-Day currently lists its ~1.2 kg seasoned rotisserie chicken at **$9.07**. Warehouse pricing may be lower.

This should remain a control condition for the real-life question: how much is an hour of optimization worth if an already-cooked chicken solves the protein problem for a few dollars?

## Evidence informing the throughput direction

Human studies support several practical design choices:

- higher-fibre solid meals can empty more slowly from the stomach than lower-fibre versions
- higher-fat meals can delay gastric emptying
- higher-viscosity shakes can produce more fullness and slower gastric emptying than thinner ones
- liquid carbohydrate generally produces less satiety than solid carbohydrate, which is useful when the goal is deliberate calorie surplus
- solids usually require a gastric lag/trituration phase; texture and particle size can influence eating and emptying behaviour

This means the ideal shake should be **energy dense but drinkable**, not a spoon-standing oatmeal paste, and the savoury component should be **soft, saucy and finely textured**.

## Prototype 002B — current draft

### Savoury: pork/soy/lentil bolognese

- dry pasta — **175 g**
- lean/medium ground pork — **150 g raw**
- textured soy granules — **50 g dry**
- red lentils — **40 g dry**
- tomato paste — **100 g-ish**
- frozen spinach — **50 g**
- frozen peas — **75 g**
- mozzarella — **60 g**
- olive oil — **20 g**
- iodized salt, garlic, chili, herbs/spices

Design logic:

- pasta carries most of the solid carbohydrate cheaply and softly
- pork provides real-meat flavour and easy chewing
- soy is reduced from the 100 g price-floor draft to 50 g so it disappears into the mince
- lentils are reduced to 40 g so they thicken the sauce rather than becoming the meal
- tomato paste keeps the sauce concentrated
- vegetables are deliberately modest
- mozzarella buys palatability and calories

### Sweet/liquid: fortified milk shake

- 3.25% milk — **500 mL**
- skim milk powder — **75 g**
- whole milk powder — **50 g**
- natural peanut butter — **60 g**
- banana — **150 g**
- blueberries — **100 g**
- water/ice only as needed to keep it genuinely drinkable

Important R&D rule: **thin beats thick**. If the shake becomes pudding, reduce peanut butter or powder concentration and move those calories elsewhere.

## Rough planning numbers

Using representative package/generic nutrition values, this draft lands around:

- **~3,200 kcal**
- **~183 g protein**
- **~35 g fibre**

Using the current aggressive price anchors:

- Maxi pasta / ground pork
- A1 small-bulk soy + lentils + mozzarella
- Wholesale Club tomato paste
- Walmart spinach / peas / skim milk powder
- Costco milk / natural peanut butter / banana / blueberries
- Walmart whole milk powder sale

estimated consumed-food cost is roughly **CAD $8.25/day before spices and small price-location differences**.

Call it **~$8.50–9/day** as a realistic target under current sale/wholesale conditions.

These are planning numbers. Prototype testing must use the exact package labels and weighed amounts.

## Why this may beat the $7.50 price-floor draft

The earlier floor used 100 g soy + 100 g lentils and more fibre-heavy volume.

002B deliberately pays a little more for:

- ground pork
- mozzarella
- concentrated dairy

In return it should:

- require much less chewing
- taste more conventional
- reduce legume/soy volume
- keep fibre around the mid-30s instead of ~60 g
- move ~1,300+ kcal into a drinkable component

That is probably a much better bulk trade.

## Throughput targets for the actual test

### Savoury
- active eating: **15–20 min**
- no dry bites
- no chewy soy chunks
- sauce should coat every piece of pasta
- final third should still taste appealing

### Shake
- drinking: **5–8 min**
- flows freely from a normal glass/blender cup
- no spoon required
- no heavy sludge at the bottom

### Whole system
- active consumption: **<30 min target**
- record fullness at 0 / 60 / 120 / 180 min
- record when another normal meal sounds appealing

## Immediate A/B tests

1. **50 g soy vs 25 g soy** — how much can disappear into meat sauce before we care?
2. **40 g lentils vs zero lentils** — does the fibre/micronutrient gain justify any heaviness?
3. **ground pork vs ground chicken/turkey vs rotisserie chicken** — taste, chew time, cost
4. **75 g skim powder vs 50 g** — protein-density versus drinkability
5. **whole milk powder vs extra peanut butter** — which raises calories with less fullness?
6. **blueberries vs pineapple** — blueberries win plant-compound density; pineapple is cheaper and vitamin-C-heavy
7. **60 g mozzarella vs 30 g** — measure whether the extra cheese earns its cost via palatability

## Current thesis

The likely winner is drifting toward:

> one soft, aggressively sauced pasta main + one thin, fortified dairy shake

The meal should feel like **pasta dinner plus milkshake**, not a survival ration assembled by spreadsheet.
