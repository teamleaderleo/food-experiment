# Ultimate Meal pricing anchor — 2026-09-07

Baseline retailer: **Walmart Canada online**.

Purpose: stop pretending the kitchen is already stocked. This snapshot treats the experiment as a from-zero grocery run and separates the **cash needed to buy the packages** from the **amount of food actually consumed in one prototype**.

Prices move by store, city, flyer, weight and promotion. Chicken and produce are especially variable. Re-check before treating this as current.

### Cacao is demoted

Walmart's current cacao-nib options are expensive; one 454 g listing was **$27.99**. Buying it there would take the stock-up to roughly **$118.12**.

Cacao stays optional until a sensible bulk / sale source appears. It is a flavour and plant-compound bonus, not a nutritional bottleneck.

This exact quantity set may undershoot the 3,000–3,500 kcal target. Scaling the cheap calorie chassis — extra rice, oats, oil and/or seeds — should put a 3,000+ kcal version around **$15–16/day** before taste-driven upgrades.

Adding avocado, cabbage, tomato and hemp at the original concept quantities pushes the meal toward roughly **$18/day**. Cacao at the Walmart price adds about another dollar per meal while requiring a ridiculous ~$28 package buy-in.

## Interesting comparison

The current $13.99 Domino's loaded-large coupon lands at $15.81 after the observed tax in the live order. That means the Ultimate Meal is competing with an unusually aggressive pizza promotion on raw daily-food dollars.

The grocery version earns its keep through fibre, fruit/vegetable diversity, micronutrient coverage, reusable pantry inventory and the ability to tune ingredients. The pizza remains an excellent control condition for calories + protein + zero cooking.

## Current price thesis

The expensive modules are easy to see:

1. chicken
2. skyr
3. berries
4. avocado / premium seeds if included

Rice, dried beans, oats and oil are extremely cheap calorie carriers. Flax and sunflower seeds are cheap enough to keep. Hemp is optional. Cacao is benched.

### Cost-optimization branches to test

- boneless thigh vs bone-in thigh vs chicken breast
- skyr vs Greek yogurt + another protein source
- canned vs dried beans after convenience is measured
- 500 g berries vs larger frozen bags
- avocado as taste upgrade rather than mandatory daily item
- sunflower + flax as default seed pair; hemp only if it earns its price
- whey / milk branch for pure protein-per-dollar testing
