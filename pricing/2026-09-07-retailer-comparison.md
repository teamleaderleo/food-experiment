## Independent Asian-supermarket reality check

Current Quebec/Montreal-area flyer data shows why the independent stores deserve their own lane rather than being treated like T&T clones:

- Kim Phat: Wing Hing soft tofu $1.69 this week; chicken legs with back around $2.29–2.39/lb in current comparison data; whole wings $3.09/lb.
- Marché C&T: fresh chicken drumsticks $1.99 in the current flyer; red pepper $1.99; several produce specials under mainstream-supermarket pricing.
- Marché Newon: current flyers show extremely aggressive chicken/produce pricing as well.

Flyer stores are volatile. Their role in the R&D plan is **opportunistic protein + produce**, checked weekly rather than used as a fixed permanent price assumption.

## Multi-store winner map

### Buy at Maxi / PC discount banner
- giant bag of white rice
- black beans when Suraj/PC price is competitive
- eggs
- mixed frozen berries if Costco/Adonis single-berry options are less useful
- generic pantry items when package size and cash outlay matter

### Buy at Costco if already a member and the package will be eaten
- oats
- Greek yogurt
- flax
- frozen blueberries
- some chicken formats when T&T/Asian-sale meat is unavailable

Costco's real in-warehouse unit price is usually lower than the Same-Day values used here.

### Buy at T&T / Chinese supermarket
- chicken thighs / legs / drumsticks when on sale
- tofu
- rice if jasmine quality is preferred
- cabbage, Chinese lettuce, bok choy, gai lan, spinach, sprouts
- mushrooms / herbs / peppers when current pricing is attractive

### Buy at independent Asian markets (Kim Phat / C&T / Newon-type stores)
- flyer chicken
- pork shoulder / other cheap meat rotations
- tofu
- leafy greens and inexpensive produce
- seafood when a weekly special gives a useful omega-3 module

### Buy at Adonis / Middle-Eastern supermarkets
- olive oil when the recurring 1L sales hit
- frozen berries when the 2kg / 600g deals are strong
- chicken when flyer pricing beats the mainstream chains
- yogurt only when sale pricing is compelling
- herbs / peppers / tomatoes / legumes when the flyer is strong

### Keep Walmart in the loop
- cheap canned sardines
- sunflower seeds
- small-package pantry items
- any ingredient where Costco bulk would create waste or a silly buy-in

## What this does to Prototype 1 cost

The Walmart-only consumed-food estimate was about **$15.16** for the original quantity set.

Using current best observed lanes for the same general ingredient quantities:

- Maxi bulk rice
- Walmart/Maxi dry black beans
- Costco oats
- Adonis-sale olive oil
- T&T sale boneless thighs
- Maxi eggs
- Costco warehouse-priced Greek yogurt
- Walmart sardines
- cheap Asian-market greens
- Adonis/Costco blueberries
- Costco flax
- Walmart sunflower seeds

produces a rough **~$11.50 consumed-food base** before any extra calories needed to hit the final 3,000–3,500 kcal target.

Even after scaling cheap rice/oats/oil upward, a repeated optimized version looks capable of landing around **$12–13/day** while preserving the high-protein/high-fibre design.

That is roughly 15–25% below the Walmart-only prototype, with the catch that the up-front package buy-in rises and the route uses multiple stores.

## Practical strategy tiers

### Tier 1 — Low cash / low complexity
Walmart + Maxi. Aim for ~$14–15/day and smallish package buy-in.

### Tier 2 — Two-store sweet spot
Walmart + T&T / independent Asian store. The big win is replacing $18/kg boneless thighs with ~$11/kg sale thighs and using cheaper fresh greens. Roughly ~$13/day territory.

### Tier 3 — Peak nerd optimization
Maxi + Costco + T&T/independent Asian + Adonis + Walmart. Roughly ~$12–13/day, highest stock-up cost, best unit economics.

## Next R&D move

Optimize **protein dollars first**. Chicken and high-protein dairy dominate the variable food cost. The most promising experiments are:

1. T&T/Asian-sale boneless thigh vs bone-in thigh/leg
2. Costco Greek yogurt vs PC Greek vs tofu blend
3. chicken + tofu split instead of all-chicken
4. sardines / mackerel as the omega-3 module only, instead of treating fish as a large protein serving
5. use cabbage / Asian greens for most plant volume and reserve berries / red pepper for micronutrient and polyphenol roles
